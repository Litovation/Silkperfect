## 1. Backtest-overfitting research (Bailey/Lopez de Prado, Harvey-Liu-Zhu, White/Hansen/Romano-Wolf, Quantopian 888, McLean-Pontiff)

### Takeaway
A t-stat of 1.5-1.8 is below even the classic 2.0 bar and well below the roughly 3.0 hurdle Harvey-Liu-Zhu propose for newly discovered factors, and Quantopian's 888-algorithm study found that backtest Sharpe explained almost none (R-squared under about 0.025) of out-of-sample Sharpe. The honest reading is "promising but not distinguishable from selection luck", unless the number of trials is small and is counted.

### Cited Findings
- Harvey, Liu and Zhu (Review of Financial Studies 2016; NBER WP 20592, 2014) argue that a newly discovered factor should clear a t-ratio above 3.0 instead of 2.0, using a multiple-testing framework with historical cutoffs from 1967 onward; they conclude most claimed findings in financial economics are likely false. — [WebSearch summary of NBER/RFS](https://nber.org/papers/w20592) (snippet-only; abstract not opened)
- The same paper is described by a later citing paper as enumerating 316 candidate factors from 313 papers (secondary count, unverified against the paper). — [NBER w20592 via search](https://nber.org/papers/w20592); [RFS entry](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:oup:rfinst:v:29:y:2016:i:1:p:5-68.)
- Bailey and Lopez de Prado, "The Deflated Sharpe Ratio: Correcting for Selection Bias, Backtest Overfitting and Non-Normality", Journal of Portfolio Management 2014. DSR corrects the Sharpe ratio for selection bias under multiple testing and for non-normal returns; the best of many trials has a different (inflated) distribution from a randomly picked one. — [SSRN 2460551](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2460551) (snippet-only)
- DSR formula as quoted by secondary sources: it is the probabilistic Sharpe ratio evaluated against a hurdle SR* = sqrt(V) x [(1-g) x Phi^-1(1-1/N) + g x Phi^-1(1-1/(N e))], with V the variance of trial Sharpe ratios, N the number of independent trials and g about 0.5772 (Euler-Mascheroni). A source also gives the minimum-backtest-length relation MinBTL about 2 ln N / E[max]^2. Overstating N makes DSR conservative; understating N makes it optimistic. — [Deflated Sharpe explainer](https://5x5x5x5.github.io/quant-lessons/backtest/deflated-sharpe/) and [Wikipedia DSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deflated_Sharpe_ratio) (snippet-only)
- The companion paper is "The Probability of Backtest Overfitting" (Journal of Computational Finance 2017); PBO is estimated with combinatorially symmetric cross-validation. — [SSRN 2326253](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2326253) (snippet-only)
- Lopez de Prado argues in Advances in Financial Machine Learning (ch. 11) that backtesting is not a research tool and that most published backtests are flawed by selection bias from multiple testing; he developed purged cross-validation with an embargo, and combinatorial purged CV builds many train/test splits while removing overlapping samples. Earlier version: "The 7 Reasons Most ML Funds Fail" (2017), expanded to 10 reasons (GARP, Jan 2018). — [GARP whitepaper](https://www.garp.org/white-paper/the-10-reasons-most-machine-learning-funds-fail); [Purged CV (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purged_cross-validation) (snippet-only)
- Quantopian ("All that Glitters Is Not Gold", Wiecki, Campbell, Lent, Stauth, 2016): 888 algorithms, each with at least 6 months out-of-sample after being backtested on the platform. Backtest Sharpe had very little predictive power (R-squared below 0.025 in one summary; 0.01-0.02 for individual in-sample metrics in another, so IS explains 1-2% of OOS behaviour). More backtesting by a quant went with a bigger IS/OOS gap. Non-linear ML trained on several IS features predicted OOS Sharpe far better (R-squared about 0.17). Reported portfolio results differ between summaries (Sharpe 1.2 in one; top-10 net Sharpe 1.8 vs 0.7 for the top in-sample picks in another) and could not be reconciled. — [Quantpedia summary via search](https://quantpedia.com/quantopians-academic-paper-about-in-vs-out-of-sample-performance-of-trading-alg/); [paper PDF mirror](https://c.mql5.com/forextsd/forum/208/All%20that%20Glitters%20Is%20Not%20Gold%20-%20Comparing%20Backtest%20and%20Out-of-Sample%20Performance%20on%20a%20Large%20Cohort%20o.pdf); [CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/big-ideas/in-sample-vs-out-of-sample-performance-of-888-trading-strategies) (all snippet-only)
- McLean and Pontiff (Journal of Finance 2016, vol. 71 no. 1, pp. 5-32) study 97 variables that predicted cross-sectional returns in academic papers, comparing in-sample, out-of-sample-pre-publication and post-publication periods. They conclude investors learn from publications and trade the effect away; trading volume and short interest rise after publication. — [EconPapers entry](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:bla:jfinan:v:71:y:2016:i:1:p:5-32); [PDF mirror](https://Www.Gwern.net/doc/economics/2016-mclean.pdf) (snippet-only)
- White's Reality Check needs the full "universe" of rules tried and can be distorted by poor or irrelevant rules (Hansen 2004), which is why Hansen's SPA test uses a studentized statistic and a less conservative null; Hansen-Lunde-Nason found calendar effects with SPA that the Reality Check (Sullivan-Timmermann-White 2001) had not. Romano-Wolf (2005) StepM adds stepwise multiple-testing control and also uses studentization. Sullivan-Timmermann-White applied the Reality Check to technical rules on about 100 years of Dow data. — [Hsu and Kuan paper mirror](https://c.mql5.com/forextsd/forum/211/Re-Examining%20the%20Profitability%20of%20Technical%20Analysis%20with%20Whitets%20Reality%20Check%20and%20Hansen9s%20SPA%20Tes.pdf); [Sheppard lecture handout](https://bashtage.github.io/kevinsheppard.com/files/teaching/mfe/advanced-econometrics/AFE_Week_4_handout.pdf); [STW PDF](https://bashtage.github.io/kevinsheppard.com/files/teaching/mfe/advanced-econometrics/Sullivan_Timmermann_White.pdf) (snippet-only)
- A 2026 critique: the trial count N is ambiguous when trials are correlated (e.g. Optuna trials), and purged CV on a small validation block (one example: a 20% final test set is about 250 bars) can still mislead; claims that CPCV beats walk-forward on PBO/DSR are mostly self-reported on historical S&P 500 data. — [mql5 unified validation pipeline](https://www.mql5.com/en/articles/21603); [mql5 article 24238](https://www.mql5.com/en/articles/24238) (blog-level, snippet-only)

### Inferences
- Own calculation (not from a source): the project's t of 1.5-1.8 over about 5.75 years implies an annualised Sharpe of the excess-over-benchmark return of only about 0.63-0.75 (t divided by sqrt of years). Reaching t of 3 at that Sharpe would need roughly 16-36 years of data.
- Own calculation using the DSR formula above, assuming an illustrative annual Sharpe of 0.9, 5.75 years of daily data, normal returns and a cross-trial Sharpe standard deviation of 0.3: DSR probability about 0.85 for 10 trials, 0.70 for 50 trials and 0.57 for 200 trials. The project has tried "many settings" (10 combinations of momentum settings plus 9 other method families plus 64 day-trading variants plus 4 AI models), so an honest N is likely in the tens to hundreds, and the DSR would sit well below the usual 0.95. These inputs are assumptions; the project should compute DSR from its actual return series and logged trial count.
- The weak 1-2% IS-to-OOS relationship from Quantopian means the 25.8% headline should be treated as an upper bound; the last two years (2025, 2026) behind holding may already be the "out-of-sample" signal.
- Momentum is a published anomaly (decades of work), so McLean-Pontiff-style decay applies: expect the live edge to be smaller than the in-sample one.

### Gaps
- Exact McLean-Pontiff decay percentages. My memory of the abstract is roughly 26% lower out-of-sample and 58% lower post-publication, but the search summary did not show these and the paper could not be opened. Treat as unverified until checked at DOI 10.1111/jofi.12365.
- Bailey et al. "Pseudo-Mathematics and Financial Charlatanism" claim (from memory): a 5-year daily backtest should not try more than about 45 independent configurations. I could not confirm it; my own quick use of the MinBTL relation above (2 ln N / SR^2 with SR=1) gives about 7.6 years for N=45, so the paper's scaling must differ from the snippet's. Do not cite either number without reading the paper.
- Harvey-Liu-Zhu exact cutoff values by year and the Romano-Wolf algorithm details; the White "7846 rules" figure I asked about returned no source.
- All primary PDFs (davidhbailey.com, SSRN, NBER) were unreachable; the network setting needs to allow them for verification.

## 2. Practical validation methods (walk-forward, purged/embargoed splits, bootstrap, Monte Carlo, plateau, regimes, costs, paper trading, track-record length)

### Takeaway
Because the project has one short period and a modest t-stat, the most useful tests are those that need no new data: bootstrap/permutation tests on the monthly return series, parameter-plateau checks, a cost stress test, DSR with an honest trial count, and a minimum-track-record calculation. Live data needed to confirm a Sharpe near 1 is on the order of 3-6 years, so paper trading for months is only an operational check, not statistical proof.

### Cited Findings
- Minimum Track Record Length (Bailey and Lopez de Prado, "The Sharpe Ratio Efficient Frontier", Journal of Risk) gives the track-record length needed to reject, at a chosen confidence, that the true Sharpe is below a threshold; implemented as `MinTrackRecord` in R PerformanceAnalytics. A 2026 arXiv paper writes the formula in terms of per-bar Sharpe, skewness and raw kurtosis at alpha = 0.05, with the kurtosis coefficient (g4-1)/4 equal to (excess kurtosis + 2)/4. — [Journal of Risk](https://www.risk.net/journal-risk/2223785/sharpe-ratio-efficient-frontier); [PerformanceAnalytics docs](https://rdrr.io/cran/PerformanceAnalytics/man/MinTrackRecord.html); [arXiv 2608.23808](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.23808) (snippet-only)
- Walk-forward advice from practitioner blogs: optimise on a window, test on the next, roll forward; "works in only 3 of 8 windows" is a sign of overfit. One blog suggests comparing live to backtest after about 100 trades and treating drift over 15-20% as overfit (single-blog heuristic, not a standard). — [DEV Community walk-forward](https://dev.to/neuzhou/your-backtest-is-lying-to-you-walk-forward-validation-catches-overfitting-1eb7); [DEV Community crypto bots](https://dev.to/trendrider/how-to-avoid-overfitting-in-crypto-trading-bots-lessons-from-10000-backtested-trades-2oci) (snippet-only, low authority)
- Paper trading is described as the final step that exposes operational problems and look-ahead bugs; no source gave a duration. — [DEV Community](https://dev.to/quantscrips/5-backtesting-mistakes-that-cost-traders-thousands-4icg) (snippet-only)
- The arch package offers IID, stationary, circular-block, moving-block and local-block bootstraps plus confidence intervals, and multiple-comparison tests SPA (Reality Check/"data snooper"), StepM and Model Confidence Set. — [arch on GitHub](https://github.com/bashtage/arch)
- skfolio ships `WalkForward(train_size=252, test_size=60)` and `CombinatorialPurgedCV(n_folds=10, n_test_folds=2)`. — [skfolio on GitHub](https://github.com/skfolio/skfolio)
- quantstats has `qs.stats.montecarlo(returns, sims=1000, bust=-0.20, goal=0.50)` with bust/goal probabilities and Monte Carlo CAGR/drawdown/Sharpe functions. — [quantstats on GitHub](https://github.com/ranaroussi/quantstats)
- backtesting.py has a built-in SAMBO optimiser (no heatmap mention found on the page). — [backtesting.py on GitHub](https://github.com/kernc/backtesting.py)
- An LLM-search paper logs every strategy evaluation and deflates Sharpe by the trial count, so the bar rises with search size (see section 5). — [arXiv 2608.27734](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.27734)

### Inferences
- Own calculation (normal returns, benchmark Sharpe 0, one-sided test, monthly data, formula n = 1 + (1 - g3 SR + (g4-1)/4 SR^2)(z/SR)^2 from the MinTRL paper, quoted from memory so verify with the R function): true annual Sharpe 1 needs about 1.8 years at 90% confidence, 2.9 years at 95%, 5.7 years at 99%. With fat tails (kurtosis 6) 95% rises to about 3.1 years, with skew -0.5 as well about 3.5 years. Sharpe 0.75 needs about 5 years at 95%; Sharpe 0.5 about 11 years. Answer to "how many months to trust Sharpe 1": about 35-40 months at 95% confidence, and this is the Sharpe of the live account, not the backtest, which is likely to be lower (see sections 1 and 3). Six months of paper trading carries almost no statistical information.
- Apply the same arithmetic to the project's own question: the relevant Sharpe is the strategy-minus-benchmark (active) Sharpe of about 0.6-0.75, which needs many more years than the strategy's own Sharpe.
- Cheap tests the project can run now with the existing return series: (a) stationary bootstrap of monthly active returns (arch) for a confidence interval on excess return; (b) permutation test, i.e. shuffle ranking signals across stocks each month and see how often random picks of 20 from 200 beat 25.8%; (c) White/SPA across all settings and methods tried, which directly addresses "many settings tried"; (d) plateau check: show the return surface across lookback/holding/top-N grid and prefer a broad plateau (the report's "ahead in all ten combinations" is a start); (e) regime splits (bull/bear years; the report shows 2025-26 is behind); (f) cost stress at 2x and 3x costs; (g) drop the best year (2021 +86.9%) and recompute; (h) DSR with N from a logged trial register. These are standard tests rather than sourced claims.
- Purged/embargoed CV matters only if the strategy fits parameters or a model on a training period; for a rule-based momentum with a handful of settings, the equivalent safeguard is to freeze the settings before the next data arrive and count all settings tried.

### Gaps
- No authoritative published guidance on required paper-trading duration was found; the number of months must be derived from MinTRL as above.
- I did not find empirical evidence (a controlled study) that walk-forward or plateau checks reduce live failure; only practitioner opinion and the Quantopian finding that IS metrics barely predict OOS.
- Stationary-bootstrap block-length choice for monthly momentum returns was not researched.

## 4. Data issues for India (survivorship, corporate actions, sources, history to 2010, cost)

### Takeaway
Survivorship bias is large in Indian small-cap indices, and NSE's own bhavcopy archives include delisted securities, so the project's choice of NSE bhavcopy is the right base. Broker APIs such as Kite do not provide delisted-instrument history. Paid vendors exist but I could only find partial price points.

### Cited Findings
- A 2026 arXiv study of NIFTY Smallcap 250 (Ranse, arXiv 2603.19380; arXiv page says March 2026, PDF dated November 2025) used 1,437 stocks over 2016-2025: current-members-only backtests gave 26.17% annualised return versus 21.23% for the full historical set, a 4.94 percentage-point (23.3% relative) overstatement, and Sharpe was overstated by 0.097 (9.1%). Of stocks that left the index, 16.1% were delisted, 33.1% graduated to larger caps and 33.2% fell below the small-cap threshold (the summary gave 82.5% as the removal rate). — [arXiv 2603.19380](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19380); [summary page](https://arxiv.deeppaper.ai/papers/2603.19380v1) (snippet-only)

## 5. LLM/agent-assisted research pitfalls (look-ahead, contamination, p-hacking by automated search, guidelines)

### Takeaway
There is now a sizeable 2026 preprint literature saying LLM-driven backtests are easily contaminated by memorised history, and that agents which search many strategy variants must deflate by the trial count; the best recent evidence found says none of the LLM-discovered strategies it tested was distinguishable from luck at honest accounting. All of this is preprint-level (not peer reviewed).

### Cited Findings
- "What survives honest evaluation? Leakage-safe, search-aware assessment of LLM-driven trading strategy discovery" (Gencay, arXiv 2608.27734, 2026): two frontier models, search budgets up to 100 candidates, five repeated runs; the system logs every strategy evaluation and deflates Sharpe by the trial count; none of the discovered strategies was distinguishable from luck under honest accounting. — [arXiv 2608.27734](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.27734); [IDEAS entry](https://ideas.repec.org/p/arx/papers/2608.27734.html) (abstract snippet only)
- "Profit Mirage" (arXiv 2510.07920): LLM financial agents can profit from memorised history rather than trading; introduces FinLake-Bench. — [arXiv 2510.07920](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.07920v1)
- Gao, Jiang and Yan (arXiv 2512.23847) give a formal test for lookahead bias in LLM forecasts and find memorisation substantially amplifies apparent predictive power. — [arXiv 2512.23847](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.23847)
- Look-Ahead-Bench (January 2026): most published LLM-based agents fail to beat baselines once past their knowledge cutoff. A "parametric look-ahead bias" paper proposes FinCAD, which cuts in-sample backtest returns by up to 67.1% on memorised dates while leaving out-of-sample close to baseline. — [arXiv 2601.13770](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.13770); [arXiv 2605.24564](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.24564) (snippet-only; attribution of each number to each paper not confirmed)
- A 2026 paper argues telling a model not to look ahead is weak protection, since a generated strategy can still read future data if the execution environment exposes it; a deliberately leaky "oracle" with Sharpe of about 35 passes DSR and PBO testing, meaning those statistics cannot detect leakage. — [arXiv 2605.24564](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.24564) (snippet-only)
- Related agent-research frameworks that search many variants: QuantEvolve (arXiv 2510.18569), AlphaSchema (arXiv 2607.26642), MadEvolve (arXiv 2605.23007), FINCOSMOS (arXiv 2611.07464). — [QuantEvolve](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.18569v1); [AlphaSchema](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.26642); [MadEvolve](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.23007); [FINCOSMOS](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.07464) (titles only)
- Note on the project's own setting: TimesFM, Chronos, Kronos and the Qlib recipe were trained on data that may include 2021-2026 prices; the project's report shows they still lost, which makes the contamination concern moot for the AI-model results but not for any future LLM-assisted signal design.

### Inferences
- When AI agents (or the team) try variants, each variant must be recorded in a trial register (date, settings, result). The DSR/SPA count N must come from that register, including the day-trading variants and failed AI models if the same data was reused to decide what to try next.
- DSR/PBO check selection and noise, not data leakage; leakage control needs a time-split design (freeze rules, then evaluate only on data after the freeze date) and code-level checks, which the project's report says it has ("automatic checks prove the future cannot leak in", 78 checks).
- Best protection against agent p-hacking: a held-out period (for example data after the freeze date, or 2010-2018 history) that the agent never sees during search.

### Gaps
- No single published, widely accepted guideline for AI-driven strategy research was found; the closest are the arXiv papers above. I could not open any of them, so the specific numbers and claims are snippet-level and several arXiv IDs have unusual dates; verify before citing.
- No evidence found about contamination of time-series foundation models (TimesFM, Chronos) specifically.

## 6. Public reviews and critiques (Reddit r/algotrading, r/quant, Quant StackExchange, blogs)

### Takeaway
Direct reviews from Reddit and Quant StackExchange were not retrievable (searches returned blogs, not those sites), so community sentiment is under-sourced here. What exists is practitioner blogs and a 2026 critique of CPCV/DSR pragmatics.
