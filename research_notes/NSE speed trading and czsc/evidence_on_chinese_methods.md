## 1. Do technical trading rules (moving average, breakout, Chan theory, candlesticks) earn returns after costs in Chinese A-shares?

### Takeaway
The peer-reviewed evidence says simple moving-average and breakout rules on Chinese indexes show statistical predictability before costs but the profit disappears once transaction costs are included; candlestick studies find some predictive patterns but do not show after-cost profit. I found no independent academic or brokerage study that validates Chan theory (缠论) at all; only code repositories and indicator scripts exist.

### Cited Findings
- Zhu, Jiang, Li and Zhou (Physica A, 2015) tested moving-average (MA) and trading-range-break (TRB, i.e. breakout) rules on the Shanghai Composite and Shenzhen indexes, sample 1992 to 2013. TRB rules beat MA rules, and short-window variable-MA rules beat long-window ones. With White's Reality Check for data snooping, the best rule beats buy-and-hold only when transaction costs are ignored; with costs, "trading profits will be eliminated completely". Result is index-level, before/after costs both reported; after-cost answer is negative. — [arXiv 1504.04254](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1504.04254); [RePEc record of Physica A 439:75-84](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/phsmap/v439y2015icp75-84.html)
- Jiang, Tong and Song (International Review of Finance, 2019) tested more than 28,000 technical signals on Chinese market data with a stepwise superior-predictive-ability test and reported that results "remain valid under the presence of transaction costs". This is the main peer-reviewed counter-example to the Zhu et al. result. I only have the abstract-level description; the size of the after-cost return, the cost level assumed and the sample period were not retrieved. — [RePEc record, IRF 19(1):191-206](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/irvfin/v19y2019i1p191-206.html)
- Chen, Bao and Zhou (Physica A, 2016) tested four pairs of two-day bullish/bearish Japanese candlestick patterns in the Chinese stock market with a Step-SPA data-snooping correction. Three of the eight patterns gave both short-term and relatively long-term prediction. The abstract does not mention transaction costs, so this is evidence of predictability, not of after-cost profit. — [RePEc record, Physica A 457:148-165](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/phsmap/v457y2016icp148-165.html); [ECNU record](https://pure.ecnu.edu.cn/en/publications/the-predictive-power-of-japanese-candlestick-charting-in-chinese-/)
- Deng et al. (SAGE Open, August 2022) tested ten candlestick patterns on SSE 50 component stocks from 2000 onward: some bullish patterns have predictive value at certain holding periods, none of the bearish patterns do; bootstrap and out-of-sample checks reported. Cost treatment not stated in the abstract. — [DOAJ record](https://doaj.org/article/1c545d4ddf834eb2a8ca41bbfb6bf695)
- Chan theory (缠论): a targeted Chinese-language search for empirical papers or brokerage (金工) reports on its effectiveness returned none. What exists is tooling: an open-source A-share Chan-theory backtest dashboard that scans about 1,940 A-shares and includes commission and stamp duty, a Python implementation of a Chan trading system, and TradingView indicator scripts. None of these reports a statistically tested return. Chan theory originates from 108 blog posts by "缠中说禅". — [github.com/zinan92/backtest](https://github.com/zinan92/backtest); [Chan-theory repo mirror](https://gh.r-mj.com/MbappeWU/Chan-theory); [TradingView script](https://cn.tradingview.com/script/QZveieft/)

### Inferences
- The two index-level studies disagree on whether costs kill the profit (Zhu et al. 2015: yes; Jiang et al. 2019: no). The difference is probably the rule universe (a few classic rules versus 28,000 signals) and cost assumptions. A cautious reading: classic textbook MA and breakout rules do not survive costs on Chinese indexes; whether some rule in a very large searched universe does is contested.
- Candlestick evidence in China stops at "some patterns predict direction"; none of the retrieved studies demonstrates net profit after stamp duty, commission and slippage. With pattern holding periods of a few days and A-share round-trip costs that include stamp duty on sells, the burden of proof is on the pattern trader.
- Chan theory has zero independent evidence behind it in what I could find. Its rules (分型, 笔, 线段, 中枢, 背驰) need discretionary choices when coded, so any backtest is exposed to look-ahead and parameter-fitting problems. It should be labelled "popular, unverified".
- The technical-rule literature is mostly on indexes and on samples ending by 2013 to 2020. It does not cover the post-2021 period in which quant funds became a large share of A-share turnover, so any edge that did exist is likely smaller now.

### Gaps
- No academic test of Chan theory found. CNKI/万方 could not be searched from this session; a Chinese master's thesis on 缠论 backtests may exist there.
- Full texts of Jiang, Tong and Song (2019), Chen et al. (2016) and Deng et al. (2022) were not read; cost assumptions and return magnitudes are missing.
- No study found on individual-stock (rather than index) MA/breakout rules in A-shares after 2020 with realistic costs and T+1 constraints.

## 2. The documented A-share anomalies: reversal, size (小市值) and its collapses, low volatility, turnover/liquidity, and weak 12-month momentum

### Takeaway
The best-supported China-specific facts are: size and an earnings-yield value factor each earned over 1% per month in 2000 to 2016 once the smallest 30% "shell" stocks are removed; individual A-shares show one-month reversal rather than 12-month momentum; and most of the 469 anomalies imported from US research fail in A-shares when microcaps are not over-weighted. The small-cap premium is real historically but fragile: the 400 smallest stocks fell about 31% in five trading days in early February 2024.

### Cited Findings
- Liu, Stambaugh and Yuan, "Size and Value in China" (Journal of Financial Economics, 2019; NBER w24458): sample 2000 to 2016. The smallest 30% of stocks are excluded from all factor construction because they are priced largely as reverse-merger shells under tight IPO constraints; these stocks are about 7% of total market capitalisation. Value is best captured by earnings-to-price (EP), which dominates book-to-market. Both the size and the value factor have average premiums above 1% per month. Size and value explain an extra 15% of monthly return variance for the typical Chinese stock beyond the market factor, against under 10% in the US. These are gross factor returns (paper portfolios, long-short, no trading costs). — [Wharton PDF](https://faculty.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2018/03/Size-and-Value-in-China.pdf); [NBER w24458](https://www.nber.org/papers/w24458.pdf); [HKU repository copy of JFE version](https://repository.hku.hk/bitstream/10722/273695/1/content.pdf)
- Same paper, working-paper draft: roughly 40% of the market value of the bottom-30% stocks reflects shell value (probability of being a reverse-merger target times the event return). This figure is from an early draft and should be checked against the published tables. — [NBER w24458](https://www.nber.org/papers/w24458.pdf)
- Same paper: replicating the US Fama-French procedure in China leaves a 17% annual alpha on the EP factor; the China three-factor model (CH-3) explains most reported Chinese anomalies including profitability and volatility anomalies, and "fails only with some of the reversal and turnover anomalies". — [Alpha Architect summary](https://alphaarchitect.com/size-and-value-in-china/); [Quantpedia summary](https://quantpedia.com/asset-pricing-models-in-china/)
- Li, Liu, Liu and Wei, "Replicating and Digesting Anomalies in the Chinese A-Share Market" (Management Science 70(8), 2024): 469 anomaly variables tested with mainboard breakpoints and value-weighted returns. 83.37% do not produce a significant high-minus-low quintile return spread; the failure rate is 84.22% on CAPM alphas and 86.99% on Fama-French three-factor alphas. The authors argue the conventional approach (all-A-share breakpoints, equal-weighted returns) over-weights microcaps and has very limited investment capacity. CH-3, CH-4 and q-factor models perform best. Gross of trading costs. — [PolyU repository PDF](https://ira.lib.polyu.edu.hk/bitstream/10397/115240/1/Li_Replicating_Digesting_Anomalies.pdf); [RePEc record](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:inm:ormnsc:v:70:y:2024:i:8:p:5066-5090)
- Momentum versus reversal, clientele evidence: "Momentum, Reversals, and Investor Clientele" (Review of Finance; PolyU authors) finds that only B-shares show momentum and earnings drift, and only A-shares show monthly reversals; the authors attribute this to A-shares being dominated by retail traders and B-shares by foreign institutions. — [Review of Finance page](https://revfin.org/momentum-reversals-and-investor-clientele/); [PolyU PDF](https://ira.lib.polyu.edu.hk/bitstream/10397/95789/1/Momentum_Reversal_and_Investment_Clientele.pdf)
- Morgan Stanley quants (as reported by Bloomberg): chasing winners over a 12-month period does not work well in China; the reversal factor was consistent across all sectors and strongest when betting against the prior one- and three-month trend. They note short-selling restrictions make it hard for foreigners to implement. Practitioner note, date not retrieved. — [BloombergQuint](https://www.bloombergquint.com/markets/morgan-stanley-quants-say-overreaction-bets-work-best-in-china)
- A 2019 paper in Research in International Business and Finance (vol. 47, pp. 78-101) finds small stocks show stronger reversals than large ones and much of the return occurs in the first month after formation. — [RePEc record](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:riibaf:v:47:y:2019:i:c:p:78-101)
- A University of Reading-hosted paper (RIBAF manuscript) using functional data analysis reports that mid-term momentum faded after the 2008 crisis and reversal took over, with reversal at 1 to 6 months and at 3 years. — [Reading repository PDF](https://centaur.reading.ac.uk/109131/8/RIBAF-D-22-00201-R2.pdf)
- Conflicting lower-quality evidence: a conference paper (Atlantis Press) reports short-term momentum and mid-term reversal concentrated at 9- and 12-month holding periods, which disagrees with the above on which horizon reverses. — [Atlantis Press PDF](https://www.atlantis-press.com/article/25865002.pdf)
- A trend factor extension (Liu, Zhou and Zhu, presented 2021) adds a fourth factor built from price and volume trend signals to explain the reversal and turnover anomalies that CH-3 misses. — [AEA 2021 conference slides](https://topcat.aeaweb.org/conference/2021/preliminary/powerpoint/3T4YbyH4)
- Size-effect collapse, early 2024: an index of China's 400 smallest listed companies ("micro-cap stocks") fell 31% in the first five trading days of February 2024, far worse than blue chips; quant funds were heavily exposed. (Reuters reporting, attribution approximate.) — [Qatar Tribune reprint](https://qatar-tribune.com/article/109660/business/chinese-hedge-funds-struggle-to-calm-investors-amid-losses)
- Lingjun's own explanation of 5 to 8 February 2024: large amounts of risk-averse money crowded into CSI 500 / CSI 1000 ETF constituents, so index constituents rose sharply relative to non-constituents; the positive feedback loop that had rewarded small-cap exposure was cut off. — [每日经济新闻, 20 Feb 2024](https://www.nbd.com.cn/articles/2024-02-20/3249462.html)

### Inferences
- The one-month reversal and the turnover-type signals are exactly the anomalies the CH-3 factor model cannot explain, and they are the raw material of the "price-volume factor" (量价因子) approach that Chinese quant funds describe. So the academic record and the funds' description of their edge point at the same thing: short-horizon mispricing created by retail order flow.
- The size premium has two parts: a shell-value part in the bottom 30% (policy-dependent: it shrinks when IPO and delisting rules change) and a cleaner premium above it. The February 2024 episode shows the bottom part can lose about a third in a week. Any backtest of an A-share strategy that is equal-weighted across all stocks is largely a bet on this part.
- The Li et al. (2024) result (about 83% to 87% of anomalies fail under value-weighting) is a strong warning that factor libraries tested on equal-weighted all-A-share universes overstate what can be traded at size.
- All academic factor numbers above are before trading costs. One-month reversal and turnover strategies have high turnover, and A-shares have limited shorting, so long-short paper returns overstate what a long-only investor captures.

### Gaps
- I did not retrieve a China-specific paper with a headline number for the low-volatility or idiosyncratic-volatility anomaly. The only sourced statement is that CH-3 "explains" the volatility anomalies, meaning a low-volatility spread exists in raw returns but is absorbed by size and EP.
- From memory, unverified in this session: the Liu-Stambaugh-Yuan paper also proposes a four-factor CH-4 model adding a turnover-based sentiment factor (PMO, "pessimistic minus optimistic", built on abnormal turnover) that prices the reversal and turnover anomalies. The search did not confirm the definition or its premium.
- No exact monthly return for the one-month reversal decile spread in A-shares was retrieved.
- No sourced full-year 2024 return for a micro-cap index (for example the Wind micro-cap index) was retrieved, nor numbers for other collapse episodes (2017 after the reverse-merger rule change).
- Jansen, Swinkels and Zhou, "Anomalies in the China A-share market" (Pacific-Basin Finance Journal) and Hou, Qiao and Zhang's replication study were searched for but not surfaced.

## 3. What the large Chinese quant funds (幻方, 九坤, 明汯, 灵均, 衍复) say they do, their excess returns 2021 to 2025, the February 2024 crisis, and the program-trading rules

### Takeaway
The main product is the index-enhanced fund (long-only, benchmarked to CSI 300/500/1000) built from short-horizon price-volume signals and machine learning across thousands of stocks; industry-level excess return on CSI 500 products was roughly 9% to 14% a year in 2021 to 2023 depending on the data vendor, sharply negative in February 2024, about 15% to 17% in 2025, and has compressed to low single digits in the first half of 2026. Firm-level strategy details (turnover, holding period, factor mix) are not publicly disclosed in any reliable source I could find.

### Inferences
- The yearly excess figures are not one consistent series. 2021 alone is quoted as 8.55% (排排网) and about 13.6% (another vendor) because of different samples (all displayed products versus selected managers) and averaging methods. The safe statement is a range: roughly 9% to 14% in 2021, about 10% in 2022, about 11% in 2023, an unknown but volatile 2024, about 15% to 17% in 2025 (much higher for CSI 1000), and about 1% to 5% in the first half of 2026.
- Excess is strongly tied to small-cap style and market turnover. It was highest in CSI 1000 products (18.58% long-run, 29.48% in 2025) and collapsed precisely when small caps collapsed (February 2024). That means a good part of reported "alpha" is exposure to the size and liquidity premia in section 2, taken by holding stocks outside the benchmark.
- The first half of 2026 compression (0.85% for CSI 500) is the most recent data point and suggests the 2025 result was a favourable-environment year, not a new normal.
- Private funds earned roughly 1.7 to 1.8 times the excess of public mutual funds on the same benchmarks over 2018 to 2025 (8.81% against 4.88% on CSI 500). Public funds face tighter tracking and turnover constraints, so this is a rough measure of what faster trading and looser constraints are worth.
- The 300 orders per second threshold is far above anything a retail or open-source user does, so the rules do not narrow the gap between professionals and retail; they mainly cap the most extreme high-frequency strategies.

### Gaps
- No reliable primary source for what 幻方, 九坤, 明汯, 灵均 or 衍复 individually disclose about factor mix, share of machine learning, holding period or turnover. Their own websites and investor letters were not reachable or not surfaced. Statements such as "holding under ten days, turnover around 100 times a year" are industry-level press descriptions.
- No per-firm excess return by year for 2021 to 2025. Such tables exist on 私募排排网 and in custodian-broker weekly sheets but are restricted to qualified investors.
- Full-year 2024 industry excess return not found. CTA and market-neutral product returns by year not researched.
- Caixin's in-depth article of 23 February 2024 (DMA size and leverage numbers) could not be opened: caixinglobal.com is not in this session's allowed network domains. Same for scmp.com and news.qq.com.
- I could not confirm whether any rule changes were made after July 2025 (for example fee schedules for high-frequency accounts actually being charged).

## 4. The gap between the funds' methods and open-source tools such as Qlib; do public factor sets (Alpha158, Alpha360, Alpha101, 国泰君安 191) still earn excess returns?

### Takeaway
I found no rigorous, independent study of whether Alpha158, Alpha360, Alpha101 or the 国泰君安 191 alphas kept earning excess returns after publication, and no on-record practitioner statement quantifying it. What exists are backtests: Qlib's own benchmark (information coefficient about 0.045, about 9% annualised excess on CSI 300) and hobbyist reproductions, none of which is live money.

### Cited Findings
- Qlib official benchmark, CSI 300 universe, Alpha158 features with LightGBM: IC 0.0448 and annualised excess return 9.01%, as quoted by a third-party project. Backtest, with Qlib's default cost settings; sample period not retrieved here. — [OpenTrade repo quoting Qlib benchmark](https://github.com/Holidayrabbit/OpenTrade); [Qlib benchmarks folder](https://www.github.com/microsoft/qlib/blob/main/examples/benchmarks)
- OpenTrade, an open-source weekly-rebalanced paper-trading experiment across four markets (A-shares, US stocks, crypto, gold) from 1 January to 25 October 2026 using Alpha158 with LightGBM/GRU, costs included: models clearly beat the benchmark in A-shares and gold; price-volume factors failed entirely in US stocks (IC about 0.004) and crypto. Its reproduced A-share IC was 0.0385. This is simulated trading by an individual project, not real capital, about ten months long. Unverified. — [github.com/Holidayrabbit/OpenTrade](https://github.com/Holidayrabbit/OpenTrade)
- Hobbyist backtests: a CSDN post reports Alpha158 with LightGBM on ten years of A-share data giving IC 0.039, 24% annualised, 10% drawdown; a Zhihu post reports 40% annualised after rewriting the Alpha158 expressions. Blog backtests, cost and universe assumptions unclear. Flag as unverified. — [CSDN](https://blog.csdn.net/weixin_38175458/article/details/126888793); [知乎](https://zhuanlan.zhihu.com/p/650339908)
- A 2026 arXiv paper describes the Qlib handcrafted factor sets as known for stability and strong performance, i.e. the academic machine-learning literature still uses them as the standard benchmark input. — [arXiv 2603.16365](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.16365)
- General decay evidence (not China, not Qlib): a single-author December 2025 preprint reports that mechanical factors (momentum, reversal) decay after publication in a way its crowding model fits, that US momentum fell from about 10% a year in the 1990s to about 2%, and that crowding predicts crashes rather than average returns. Preprint, provisional. — [arXiv 2512.11913](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.11913v1)
- Indirect professional-versus-constrained comparison: over 2018 to end-2025, private quant funds' annualised excess was 6.61% / 8.81% / 18.58% on CSI 300 / 500 / 1000 against 3.90% / 4.88% / 11.06% for public index-enhanced mutual funds. — [东方证券 report via 发现报告](https://www.fxbaogao.com/detail/5283839)
- Industry-level description of professional practice: holdings under ten days, thousands of stocks, turnover of about 100 times a year. — [腾讯新闻, 6 Dec 2023](https://news.qq.com/rain/a/20231206A00DQO00)
- Capacity warning from academia: equal-weighted, all-A-share anomaly tests over-weight microcaps and have very limited investment capacity; under value-weighting about 83% of 469 anomalies fail. — [Li, Liu, Liu and Wei, PolyU PDF](https://ira.lib.polyu.edu.hk/bitstream/10397/115240/1/Li_Replicating_Digesting_Anomalies.pdf)

### Inferences
- The Qlib headline of about 9% annualised excess on CSI 300 from public daily price-volume features is the same order as the industry average private-fund excess on CSI 300/500 (6.6% to 8.8% a year over 2018 to 2025). That does not mean the public features are as good as the funds: the Qlib number is a backtest with idealised fills, no capacity limit, no market impact, daily-bar execution at assumed prices, and a model family tuned on a benchmark that hundreds of papers have since fitted to. The fund number is realised, after trading costs, at tens of billions of yuan.
- The gap, by dimension, as far as the evidence allows:
  - Data: Alpha158 and Alpha360 are built only from daily open/high/low/close/volume/VWAP. Funds describe using intraday and order-book (tick, level-2) data and alternative data; I have no primary source quantifying this.
  - Speed: professionals rebalance continuously intraday with algorithmic execution (holding under ten days, about 100 times annual turnover); Qlib's default workflow is daily-bar signals with next-day execution. The 300-orders-per-second regulatory threshold shows the scale of professional order flow.
  - Costs: a retail user pays stamp duty, commission and the full spread; at about 100 times turnover a difference of even 0.05% per side is about 10% a year, which is the whole average excess. This is arithmetic, not a sourced figure.
  - Scale: small accounts have one real advantage, namely capacity. Microcap and illiquid signals that Li et al. show to be untradeable at institutional size remain reachable for small money, with the February 2024 type of tail risk attached.
- Public alpha sets are best treated as feature libraries and teaching material, not as strategies with a demonstrated live record. The compression of professional CSI 500 excess to under 1% in the first half of 2026 suggests simple daily price-volume signals, which are the most crowded, have little left on the liquid benchmarks.

### Gaps
- No practitioner quotations (fund managers, brokerage 金工 teams) on Alpha158/Alpha360/Alpha101/GTJA191 decay were found. Chinese brokerage factor-tracking reports (华泰, 国泰君安/国泰海通, 光大 金工) very likely contain year-by-year IC and long-short returns for these sets, but none surfaced in search and brokerage portals are not openly reachable.
- No out-of-sample study of WorldQuant "101 Formulaic Alphas" (Kakushadze, 2016) or the 国泰君安 191 short-horizon price-volume alphas (2017 report) in A-shares after their publication dates was found.
- No sourced numbers on professional data costs (level-2 feeds, colocation), commission rates for quant funds, or realised market impact.
- Qlib benchmark sample periods and cost settings were not read directly from the repository in this session.

## 5. Do Chinese-developed methods transfer to other markets such as India?

### Takeaway
There is no published evidence, academic or practitioner, that A-share-derived factor models or Chan-theory-style methods work on Indian stocks. What is documented is that Chinese quant firms intend to trade India, and that commentators warn against copying one market's template into another.

### Cited Findings
- Reuters (April 2024): Shanghai-based Minghong Investment was preparing strategies targeting Japan and India after testing the US and South Korea with its own money; another Chinese firm planned to start in Asian markets such as Japan, India and Thailand before Europe and the US. Motive given: competition and tighter rules at home. These are stated plans, not results. — [Reuters via Investing.com](https://investing.com/news/economy/chinese-quant-funds-expand-abroad-as-rules-tighten-at-home-3387771); [DealStreetAsia](https://www.dealstreetasia.com/stories/chinese-quant-funds-set-foot-abroad-as-rules-tighten-at-home-393140)
- A Chinese economist quoted by the South China Morning Post argued Chinese quants have an edge over foreign rivals at home, but that "if a top US quant team goes to Japan or India, they can beat virtually all the native quants". Opinion; page not fetched. — [SCMP](https://www.scmp.com/business/banking-finance/article/3363610/chinas-quant-funds-have-edge-foreign-rivals-even-beijing-curbs-trading-speed)
- Business Standard (August 2023), headline "Quantitative strategists fail to replicate US and China success in India": quant assets are under 1% of the total in India against about 35% in the US; an M&G fund manager said quant "hasn't grown because traditional stock picking does work in India". — [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/markets/news/quantitative-strategists-fail-to-replicate-us-and-china-success-in-india-123082500137_1.html)
- A venture investor quoted in AVCJ on China-India investing generally: "Mistakes get made when investors in general try copy and paste templates from one market to another"; success in China does not guarantee success in India. Not about quant trading specifically. — [AVCJ](https://www.avcj.com/avcj/analysis/3008937/china-india-tech-investments-a-running-start)
- Cross-market data point from a hobby project: the same Alpha158 price-volume pipeline that beat the benchmark in A-shares in 2026 paper trading had IC of about 0.004 in US stocks, i.e. it did not transfer. India was not tested. Unverified simulation. — [github.com/Holidayrabbit/OpenTrade](https://github.com/Holidayrabbit/OpenTrade)
- Why the A-share pattern may be market-specific: within China itself, only A-shares (retail-dominated) show monthly reversal while B-shares (foreign-institution-dominated) show momentum. — [Review of Finance: Momentum, Reversals, and Investor Clientele](https://revfin.org/momentum-reversals-and-investor-clientele/)

### Inferences
- The A-share versus B-share result implies that short-term reversal and price-volume signals depend on who trades, not on being "Chinese". Whether they transfer to India depends on whether the relevant Indian segment is retail-driven in the same way. Indian cash-equity small and mid caps and the options market have very high retail participation, so some transfer is plausible, but that is a hypothesis with no test behind it in the sources found.
- Structural differences that would change results: India allows intraday round trips in cash equities and has a deep single-stock and index derivatives market, whereas A-shares are T+1 with a 10%/20% daily price limit and restricted shorting. The shell-value component of the Chinese size premium is specific to China's IPO regime and has no reason to exist in India.
- The only cross-market evidence (Alpha158 failing in US stocks) points against assuming transfer by default.

### Gaps
- No academic paper applying China-specific factor models (CH-3/CH-4), Alpha158-type features, or Chan theory to NSE/BSE stocks was found.
- No reported results from any Chinese quant firm's India strategy. The Minghong plan dates from April 2024; whether it launched is unknown.
- The Business Standard and SCMP articles were not opened in full; only search-level summaries were available.
- Indian-market studies of one-month reversal, momentum and turnover effects (which exist in the Indian finance literature) were outside the search budget and are not covered here.
