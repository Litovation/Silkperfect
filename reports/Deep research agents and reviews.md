# Deep research agents work, but verify everything

**Deep research agents are now genuinely useful for broad, cited background research, and no single product or model is a verified winner as of November 2026.** The best-documented technique is Anthropic's orchestrator-worker design: a lead agent plans, parallel subagents search, and a separate pass checks citations. Anthropic reports that this beat a single agent by 90.2% on its own internal eval, at roughly 15x the token cost of chat ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system)). Every major lab ships a "deep research" mode (OpenAI, Google, Anthropic, Perplexity, xAI, Moonshot, Alibaba), and open-weight agents such as Tongyi DeepResearch and MiroThinker let a small team self-host. Headline benchmark scores are almost all vendor self-reports, and BrowseComp-style scores near 90% are losing discriminating power. Independent audits find that even good systems make unsupported claims and bad citations at meaningful rates. Public reviewers (Simon Willison, Ethan Mollick, several tech-press hands-ons) are positive on utility and consistently say to verify. User complaints on Trustpilot centre on credits, quotas and refunds rather than research quality. For a solo founder using Claude Code, the practical answer is a layered stack: one $20 general plan or the Claude Code subagent workflow for the main loop, a second vendor to cross-check, a search API plus a page reader for web access, and Agent Reach for social and video sources. **Agent Reach is not installed in this environment** (details below). **Evidence caveat for the whole report:** the researchers could reach almost no primary sites (arxiv.org, openai.com, Reddit, Hacker News, Trustpilot and most review sites failed DNS), so most figures below come from search-result summaries, vendor self-reports or secondary blogs. Each section labels what is vendor-reported, independent, or secondary, and none of the numbers should be treated as confirmed.

## Evidence quality: what was and was not verified

Only a few sources were read in full: Anthropic's multi-agent engineering post, the Claude Research support page, and about two dozen GitHub repository pages (read on 2026-11-24). Everything else reached the researchers as snippets. That includes the CJR/Tow Center audit, the arXiv papers, G2 and Trustpilot scores, and every OpenAI and Google claim. No Reddit, Hacker News or X thread was retrieved at all, so "what users say" is built from review sites, blogs and GitHub issues, not from forum discussion. Several model names in 2026 leaderboard aggregators ("GPT-5.6 Sol", "Atria Dawn Preview", "Kimi K3.1 Pro", "GPT-6 Astra") could not be matched to any primary vendor page, so this report does not rank by them. Many "best AI for research" roundups are affiliate or competitor content, and the notes flag cases such as Zemith (which sells a competing product) supplying Trustpilot statistics. Pricing and quota figures conflict across sources for nearly every vendor. Treat every number below as "reported", not "confirmed".

## Who is doing deep research well, and what independent evidence exists

The field splits into three groups. The first is chat-integrated research modes from labs. OpenAI's Deep Research launched in February 2025 and was reported to move to GPT-5.2 around February 2026, adding connected apps, site restriction and mid-run steering (secondary reporting, [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/openais-deep-research-now-runs-on-gpt-5-2-and-lets-users-search-specific-websites/)). Google exposed its Gemini Deep Research agent to developers through the Interactions API in public beta in December 2025, and open-sourced the DeepSearchQA benchmark ([Google blog](https://blog.google/technology/developers/deep-research-agent-gemini-api/)). Perplexity launched an Advanced Deep Research for Max users in early 2026 running on Claude Opus 4.5 inside its own harness, and published the DRACO benchmark ([TestingCatalog](https://www.testingcatalog.com/perplexity-launches-advanced-deep-research-for-max-users)). Anthropic's Research feature is available on Pro, Max, Ultra, Team and Enterprise plans, and a support page read in November 2026 says it runs on Claude Opus 5.5 with a fallback to Opus 4.8 ([Claude support](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/11088861-using-the-research-feature)). The second group is credit-based "super agents" that bundle research with execution: Manus, Genspark and Perplexity Computer. The third is enterprise and API infrastructure such as You.com ARI, Exa Websets (entity-list building rather than reports) and Alibaba's Qwen Deep Research ([Alibaba Cloud docs](https://www.alibabacloud.com/help/en/model-studio/qwen-deep-research)).

On benchmarks, nearly every "we win" claim comes from the benchmark's author, so weight it accordingly. OpenAI reported 51.5% on BrowseComp at launch, while saying its model was trained on data that teaches BrowseComp-style tasks ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/browsecomp/), seen via snippet). Google's agent reported 59.2% on BrowseComp and 66.1% on DeepSearchQA ([Analytics India Mag](https://analyticsindiamag.com/ai-news-updates/googles-new-deep-research-agent-scores-sota-results-on-benchmarks)). Perplexity's DRACO has Perplexity at 70.5%, Gemini 59.0% and OpenAI o3 52.1%, but Perplexity wrote the benchmark ([Digital Citizen](https://www.digitalcitizen.life/perplexity-launches-advanced-deep-research-for-max-users-with-draco-ai-research-benchmark/)). You.com reported a 76% win rate against OpenAI Deep Research on its own DeepConsult test ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/ai/you-coms-ari-enterprise-crushes-openai-in-head-to-head-tests-aims-at-deep-research-market)). The best academic report-quality evidence is the DeepResearch Bench RACE score, where Gemini-2.5-Pro Deep Research led the 2025 paper at 48.88 ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.11763)). That data is 6 to 12 months stale, the judge model has since changed, and the current leaderboard top could not be loaded, so old and new scores are not comparable ([GitHub](https://github.com/Ayanami0730/deep_research_bench)).

At the model level, 2026 BrowseComp figures cluster around 84 to 93%. Examples are OpenAI's own launch table (GPT-5.5 at 84.4%, GPT-5.5 Pro at 90.1%, with Claude Opus 4.7 at 79.3% and Gemini 3.1 Pro at 85.9%) and an OpenRouter listing of Claude Opus 5 with Perplexity search over 25 turns at 89.0% ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/introducing-gpt-5-5/), via snippet; [OpenRouter](https://openrouter.ai/benchmarks/browsecomp)). These are lab-reported, there is no official BrowseComp leaderboard, and multi-agent or large-budget runs sit beside single-agent runs on some boards ([Scholar Compass](https://scholarcompass.net/en/tasks/deep-research-agents/benchmarks/browsecomp)). The more credible signals are the controlled ones. LiveBrowseComp reportedly shows search-augmented scores falling 25 to 40 points versus BrowseComp ([arXiv 2605.28721](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.28721)). BrowseComp-Plus, which fixes the corpus, showed GPT-5 rising from 55.9% to 70.1% simply by swapping the BM25 retriever for a dense embedding retriever ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.06600)). A 2025 study also found that for about 3% of Humanity's Last Exam questions, search agents could find a public copy with the answer, and a GitHub issue on Tongyi DeepResearch reports answer leakage via search tools ([arXiv 2508.13180](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.13180v1); [GitHub issues](https://github.com/Alibaba-NLP/DeepResearch/issues)). The honest reading is that frontier systems are strong at hard fact-finding in the lab, differences among the top tier are within noise, and real-web performance is lower than the headline numbers.

On the open-source side, the picture is more mixed. The main report-writing frameworks are GPT-Researcher (about 30.9k stars, Apache-2.0), DeerFlow 2.0 from ByteDance (about 85.7k stars, now a general "SuperAgent" harness rather than a research-report tool), Stanford STORM (about 31.9k, last news January 2025), and LangChain's open_deep_research (now archived) ([GPT-Researcher](https://github.com/assafelovic/gpt-researcher); [DeerFlow](https://github.com/bytedance/deer-flow); [STORM](https://github.com/stanford-oval/storm); [open_deep_research](https://github.com/langchain-ai/open_deep_research)). Star counts are a poor quality signal here. Open-weight agent models include Tongyi DeepResearch 30B-A3B (vendor-reported BrowseComp 43.4, or 58.3 in its "Heavy" mode) and MiroThinker v1.7 (vendor-reported BrowseComp 74.0) ([Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2510.24701); [MiroThinker](https://github.com/MiroMindAI/MiroThinker)). Large general open-weight models such as Kimi K2.6 report 83.2 on BrowseComp on their own model card ([Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/moonshotai/Kimi-K2.6)). I found no independent leaderboard numbers for GPT-Researcher, DeerFlow or STORM, and GitHub issues show reproduction trouble for Tongyi's published results (issues #218 and #223) ([GitHub issues](https://github.com/Alibaba-NLP/DeepResearch/issues)). For report-writing frameworks, quality is governed mostly by the backing LLM: in LangChain's own table the GPT-5 configuration scored best at 0.4943 versus 0.4309 for the default, and a full 100-task run cost roughly $20 to $190 depending on configuration ([open_deep_research](https://github.com/langchain-ai/open_deep_research)).

## The methods that produced measurable gains are parallelism, tokens and better retrieval

The best-documented methods are all vendor-reported, but they agree with each other. In Anthropic's design, a lead agent writes its plan to memory, spawns three to five subagents in parallel, and a separate CitationAgent attributes claims before the report. The setup beat single-agent Opus 4 by 90.2% on an internal eval ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system)). The most important analytical finding in that post is that token usage alone explained 80% of the variance in BrowseComp performance, and three factors (tokens, tool calls, model choice) explained 95%. That suggests much of the multi-agent gain is more compute spent in parallel, which also explains the roughly 15x cost versus chat. Other documented practices include effort-scaling rules in the lead prompt (1 agent and 3 to 10 tool calls for simple lookups, 2 to 4 subagents for comparisons, more than 10 for complex work), delegation briefs with an objective, output format and boundaries, short broad queries before narrow ones, and subagents that write findings to files and pass back references. Anthropic's human testers also caught the system preferring SEO content farms over authoritative sources, a real failure mode for any web research agent. Anthropic's own caveat is that the approach suits breadth-first questions and fits poorly where agents need shared context.

Three further levers have supporting evidence. First, context management: Anthropic reported that a memory tool plus context editing improved an internal agentic-search eval by 39% and that context editing cut tokens by 84% in a 100-turn web-search eval, both self-reported ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/context-management)). Second, retrieval quality, as the BrowseComp-Plus result above shows. Third, post-training for search. Reinforcement learning on hard synthetic questions produced the open models (Kimi-Researcher reported HLE rising from 8.6% to 26.9% through RL, and Tongyi reached BrowseComp 43.4 with 3.3B active parameters), and DR Tulu from Ai2 reports matching OpenAI Deep Research on long-form benchmarks at about $0.0019 per query versus about $1.80 ([Moonshot](https://moonshotai.github.io/Kimi-Researcher); [arXiv 2511.19399](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.19399)). All of those are author-reported, none were independently reproduced in what I found, and small search models trained on simple QA transfer poorly to hard browsing (Search-R1 reached 3.86% on BrowseComp-Plus with BM25 versus 55.9% for GPT-5). For a Claude Code user, the RL results are signals about what to prompt for, not something to replicate.

Community Claude Code patterns mirror Anthropic's recipe: subagents write markdown notes into a shared folder, and a report-writer agent reads them, with the filesystem as the handoff ([PromptLayer](https://blog.promptlayer.com/using-claude-code-to-run-deep-research/)). These write-ups are anecdotal. No controlled study compares skill-based Claude Code research workflows against the benchmarks above, so any such workflow should be treated as unbenchmarked.

## How reliable the outputs are, and what users and reviewers say

The independent audits are the sobering part. The Tow Center/CJR audit of eight AI search tools (1,600 queries, March 2025) found more than 60% of answers incorrect overall, with Perplexity lowest at 37% and Grok-3 Search highest at 94% ([CJR](https://www.cjr.org/tow_center/we-compared-eight-ai-search-engines-theyre-all-bad-at-citing-news.php)). It tested news-article attribution, not full deep research, and OpenAI called it an "atypical test" ([Digital Trends](https://digitaltrends.com/computing/chatgpt-search-struggles-with-accuracy-research-shows)). DeepTRACE (Microsoft Research and Salesforce authors) measured citation accuracy of 40 to 80% across systems and found Perplexity Deep Research at 97.5% unsupported statements, while a third-party listing put GPT-5 deep research at 12.5% (the listing is unverified against the paper) ([arXiv 2509.04499](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.04499); [alphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/benchmarks/microsoft/deeptrace)). An April 2026 preprint found hallucinated URL rates of 3 to 13% across commercial systems, with deep research agents among the worst despite citing more, and an open-source `urlhealth` loop cut non-resolving URLs to under 1% ([arXiv 2604.03173](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.03173)). A May 2026 paper benchmarked 14 LLMs on citation quality and found link validity above 94% for the best but only 39 to 77% factual accuracy of the cited content ([arXiv 2605.06635](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.06635)). The Tow and DeepTRACE results conflict on Perplexity because they measure different things (news-article identification versus claim-level support) and ran different modes, so neither is a verdict on that product. The shared lesson is that a working link does not mean the page supports the claim. Security is a separate risk: studies report that agents are susceptible to indirect prompt injection in ordinary web content in roughly 25% of tasks on average (TRAP) and at 42 to 68% attack success in another benchmark, and that deep research systems can be poisoned through user-generated content such as Reddit and Wikipedia ([arXiv 2512.23128](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.23128); [arXiv 2605.24245](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.24245)). These figures came through search summaries only.

Individual reviewers are positive but guarded. Simon Willison wrote that AI-assisted search-based research "actually works now" and, at the OpenAI launch, warned that a heading-and-citation-heavy document can create a misleading impression of a PhD-level assistant ([Substack](https://simonw.substack.com/p/ai-assisted-search-based-research); [Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2025/Feb/25/deep-research-system-card/)). His test of the o4-mini-deep-research API cost about 53 cents and did not return a comprehensive list ([Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2025/Oct/18/o4-mini-deep-research/)). Ethan Mollick, per a third-party summary of his February 2026 guide, calls Deep Research and connecting AI to your own content the most important additional features, and rates OpenAI and Anthropic ahead of Google on "harness capability" ([summary](https://velikov-mihail.github.io/ai-econ-wiki/summaries/guide-which-ai)). A July 2026 remark that Gemini "has fallen off" his list is second-hand and unverified ([Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2026/jul/27/an-opinionated-guide-to-which-ai-to-use-to-do-stuff)). TechRadar judged Claude's Research "just as good as" ChatGPT and Gemini ([TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/computing/artificial-intelligence/i-tried-claudes-new-research-feature-and-its-just-as-good-as-chatgpt-and-google-geminis-deep-research-features)), PCMag named ChatGPT the winner of its comparison (snippet only) ([Yahoo/PCMag](https://tech.yahoo.com/ai/articles/chatgpt-vs-claude-ive-tested-130115195.html)), and early Gemini hands-ons called it "hit and miss" ([Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/hands-on-gemini-deep-research-3508607)). The common division of labour in blogs is Perplexity for fast cited answers, ChatGPT for long structured reports, Claude for synthesis and your own documents, and Gemini for Google-ecosystem work. Those are opinion roundups with commercial incentives, not measurements, and rankings flip with test design ([Superkind](https://superkind.ai/blog/ai-deep-research-tools)). Independent evidence on Kimi-Researcher, Grok DeepSearch, Genspark research mode, GPT-Researcher and Tongyi from reviewers is essentially absent.

Review-site data show a split. Perplexity scores about 4.4 of 5 on G2 across about 313 reviews, with complaints about usage limits (63 reviews) and inaccurate data (34 reviews), while Trustpilot is hostile, at about 1.5 from 674 reviews by one aggregator ([G2](https://g2.com/products/perplexity/reviews); [Zemith](https://www.zemith.com/en/learn/perplexity-review)). Manus scores about 4.3 on G2 but reports of 88% one-star reviews on Trustpilot, with complaints about credit consumption, deleted credits and automated refund replies ([Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/manus.im?page=1); [Preuve](https://preuve.ai/blog/manus-ai-review)). Genspark sits at 2.9 to 3.5 on Trustpilot with credits that burn fast and poor support ([Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/genspark.ai?page=9)). The pattern is that complaints concern billing and quotas rather than research quality, and the G2/Trustpilot gap likely reflects selection effects, since Trustpilot attracts disputes. Counts differ between snapshots and several come from competitors. Perplexity also faces pending lawsuits (Dow Jones/NY Post, the New York Times, Britannica and others), and no ruling was found ([Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/ip-law/news-outlets-perplexity-ai-suits-strike-at-existential-threat)). Manus has the added uncertainty that Meta agreed to buy it in December 2025 and China reportedly ordered the deal unwound on 27 April 2026, with no confirmed outcome after that ([TechNode](https://technode.com/2026/04/27/manus-related-foreign-investment-deal-ordered-unwound-in-china/)). Traffic for both Manus and Genspark has reportedly declined since launch ([KrASIA](https://kr-asia.com/chinese-ai-agents-made-a-splash-in-2025-but-can-they-go-the-distance)).

## Agent Reach is not installed here, but it is a cheap add for social and video sources

The Agent Reach skill the user believed was installed was **not found in this environment**. I checked for a `~/.agent-reach` directory (the notes say everything it installs lives there), for an `agent-reach` command on the PATH, and for any file or folder named `agent-reach` anywhere on the filesystem. None exist, and the synced skills folder contains only the deep-research skill. It needs to be installed.

Agent Reach (repo `Panniantong/Agent-Reach`, MIT licence) is best understood as an installer and router, not a new scraper. It installs and health-checks existing open-source readers (yt-dlp, bili-cli, OpenCLI, Jina Reader, Exa and others) and gives an agent one CLI to search and read Twitter/X, Reddit, YouTube, GitHub, Bilibili, XiaoHongShu, LinkedIn and the open web with no API fees. It is read-only: the README says it never posts, comments, likes or sends messages ([GitHub](https://github.com/Panniantong/Agent-Reach)). The repo page showed about 114.3k stars and 10.3k forks when fetched on 2026-11-24, but other pages quote 22.8k and 52.9k, so star counts are time-dependent and unverified. No release version or date was visible. The main documentation is in Chinese, with an English README at `docs/README_en.md`.

To install it, the repo's recommended route is to paste a sentence into your agent (Claude Code, Codex and others are named): "帮我安装 Agent Reach：" followed by the raw GitHub URL of `docs/install.md` on the main branch. The notes describe this URL but did not capture it verbatim, so take the exact link from the repo README rather than from this report ([README landing text](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Panniantong/Agent-Reach/main/docs/README_en.md)). The agent needs permission to run commands and may install `uv` without an admin password. The same command installs and upgrades. **Install only from GitHub**: the repo warns that the PyPI package called `agent-reach` is not this project, even though several secondary pages show a `pip install agent-reach` line, which contradicts that warning and should not be trusted ([GitHub](https://github.com/Panniantong/Agent-Reach)). After installing, Claude can call the tool through Bash. The documented commands are `agent-reach channels` to list capabilities, `agent-reach <channel> <action> [args]` such as `agent-reach youtube search "LLM agents"`, and `agent-reach read <url>`, which picks the channel from the URL. Secondary pages describe an `agent-reach doctor` health check, which I could not verify from the repo. Keys such as a Groq or OpenAI key for YouTube speech-to-text should be entered in your own terminal via `agent-reach configure groq-key`, not in chat. I found no documented MCP server, so the CLI-through-Bash route is the one confirmed path.

Channel tiers matter for planning. GitHub, YouTube, general web, V2EX and Bilibili work by default. Reddit, LinkedIn, XiaoHongShu and a few Chinese platforms are optional and need a login. Twitter/X, Facebook and Instagram are labelled experimental and "may stop working at any time". Login channels work by copying your login cookies from your Chrome profile into a localhost-only headless browser, and the repo recommends a secondary account because the copied login is usually your main one ([GitHub](https://github.com/Panniantong/Agent-Reach)). Reviewers flag platform terms of service and ban risk ([DEV review](https://dev.to/alya_mahalini_f05d9953cfa/agent-reach-a-pragmatic-review-of-internet-access-for-ai-agents-58kn)). Because it is a router, its reliability equals that of the weakest upstream tool per platform. Coverage of it is almost entirely promotional how-to content: I found no Hacker News or Reddit reaction, no security audit and no reliability data, so its practical value is unproven beyond the README's claims.

## A practical stack for a solo founder using Claude Code

The notes support a layered approach, with the caveat that the exact combination below is my synthesis and not a cited practitioner recommendation. Start with the main research loop. The cheapest real entry point is one $20 plan from OpenAI, Google, Anthropic or Perplexity, though quotas change often and sources conflict on current limits, so check the checkout page. Claude's Research feature draws on the same usage limits as normal chat and drains them faster ([Claude support](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/11088861-using-the-research-feature)). For custom or repeated research in Claude Code, copy Anthropic's pattern: a lead that writes a plan file and picks an effort tier, parallel subagents that each write findings and gaps to a notes folder, then a verification pass. Budget for roughly 15x chat token cost and use a cheaper model for subagents as Anthropic did (Sonnet subagents under an Opus lead).

For web access, the independent comparison available (AIMultiple, May 2026 snapshot) found Brave, Firecrawl, Exa and Tavily statistically indistinguishable on agent score except for Brave ahead of Tavily, and Firecrawl, Parallel and Tavily each publish comparisons that rank themselves first ([guptadeepak summary](https://guptadeepak.com/research/web-search-apis-ai-agents-2026/); [Firecrawl](https://www.firecrawl.dev/blog/exa-alternatives)). Pricing from third-party summaries is roughly $5 per 1,000 requests for Brave and $4 to $12 per 1,000 for Exa depending on mode, and these are unverified against vendor sites ([digitalapplied](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/web-search-apis-for-ai-agents-compared-2026)). Page text can cost 6 to 20 times the search fee at frontier-model prices, so fetching less matters more than choosing the cheapest search endpoint ([beri.net](https://www.beri.net/article/tavily-vs-exa-vs-brave-vs-perplexity-search-api-agent-cost-2026)). For reading pages, Crawl4AI is Apache-2.0 and self-hostable, and Firecrawl offers an MCP server with 1,000 free credits per month ([Crawl4AI](https://github.com/unclecode/crawl4ai); [Firecrawl](https://github.com/firecrawl/firecrawl)). For login-walled or JavaScript-heavy pages, Playwright MCP installs in Claude Code with `claude mcp add playwright npx @playwright/mcp@latest`, and its extension mode reuses your logged-in Chrome, which exposes your accounts and which its own README says is not a security boundary ([Playwright MCP](https://github.com/microsoft/playwright-mcp)). Cloud stealth browsers (browser-use Cloud at about $0.06 per browser-hour, or Browserbase) are the last resort when blocked. Prefer human-verified benchmarks such as Online-Mind2Web over vendor WebVoyager scores: a March 2026 audit reportedly gave Operator 68.6% versus the 87% OpenAI reported ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.29020), via snippet).

Self-hosting is a control and cost play, not a day-one quality win. GPT-Researcher is the easiest: `pip install gpt-researcher` with an OpenAI-compatible key and a Tavily key, about $0.08 and one minute for a standard report and about $1.30 and 7 to 12 minutes for a deep one per its README ([GPT-Researcher](https://github.com/assafelovic/gpt-researcher)). Tongyi DeepResearch and MiroThinker need Serper and Jina keys plus a GPU, and the open-weight models save LLM cost, not search cost. Hardware requirements were not stated in the READMEs. Legal and security hygiene is part of the stack. Contract-based scraping claims have fared poorly in court (X Corp v. Bright Data), but DMCA anti-circumvention claims have more traction, and a judge largely declined to dismiss Reddit's suit against SerpApi and Perplexity on 2026-07-31, so avoid CAPTCHA-solving and residential proxies against explicit blocks and avoid logged-in automation on accounts you cannot lose ([ZwillGen](https://www.zwillgen.com/alternative-data/how-artificial-intelligence-shaping-web-scraping-litigation/); [Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/ip-law/reddit-advances-ai-scraping-suit-against-perplexity-serpapi)). This is not legal advice. Treat all fetched web text as untrusted data and give browsing subagents minimal tools (no write or execute) to limit prompt-injection damage.

The recommended stack, in order of adoption, is as follows. Use one $20 general plan as the primary research tool and a Claude Code subagent workflow for custom jobs. Add a second vendor's deep research to cross-check any high-stakes question, and diff the claims, because disagreements locate hallucinations cheaply. Add Brave or Exa for search, Crawl4AI or Firecrawl for page text, Agent Reach for X, Reddit, YouTube and Bilibili sources (after verifying the install), and Playwright MCP only for pages that need your login. Add a mechanical verification pass that resolves every cited URL and checks that the quoted claim appears on the page. Use Exa Websets or similar only for prospect and company lists, and skip the credit-metered super agents (Manus, Genspark) unless you specifically want research plus deliverables and accept unpredictable billing.

**What to verify by hand before relying on this report:**

1. Current quotas and prices on the official pricing pages of OpenAI, Google, Anthropic, Perplexity, Manus and Genspark, since sources conflict and limits changed repeatedly in 2026.
2. Agent Reach: the exact `docs/install.md` URL and install steps in the repo, the `doctor` command, its current star count and last-commit date, and whether an MCP wrapper exists.
3. The primary pages behind the key audits (CJR Tow Center, DeepTRACE arXiv 2509.04499, arXiv 2604.03173 and 2605.06635), since figures were read from snippets only.
4. The 2026 model names and BrowseComp scores (GPT-5.6, "Atria Dawn Preview", Kimi K3.1, Claude Opus 5 and 5.5), which could not be matched to primary vendor pages.
5. The OpenAI BrowseComp 51.5% and GPT-5.5 launch table on openai.com, and the current RACE leaderboard top on DeepResearch Bench.
6. Reddit and Hacker News threads on Claude Code research workflows, Agent Reach, Tongyi and GPT-Researcher, none of which were reachable.
7. The reported ChatGPT Atlas discontinuation (a single low-quality-source claim) and the Manus/Meta outcome after April 2026.
8. The status of Reddit v. SerpApi/Perplexity and Google v. SerpApi dockets, where sources conflict.
9. Spot-check ten random citations from any report you intend to act on, by opening the page and finding the quoted claim.

## Conclusion

The notes change the usual question from "which deep research tool is best" to "which verification habit is cheapest". No evidence found here separates the top tier of products reliably, but every audit agrees that citation-heavy output hides unsupported claims, and the one mitigation with measured effect is mechanical: resolving URLs and checking claims against page text. That makes the verification pass, not the choice of vendor, the highest-leverage part of a solo founder's stack, and it argues for owning that step inside Claude Code rather than outsourcing trust to a product.

The second implication is about cost and control. Token spend, not architecture, appears to drive much of the multi-agent gain, and retrieval quality moves scores as much as the agent does, so good search and fetch tools are worth more than a fancier orchestrator. Open-weight and self-hosted options save model fees but not search fees, and they lack independent benchmarks. Until independent leaderboards catch up with 2026 models, and until the blocked primary sources are checked directly, treat every figure in this report as a lead to confirm rather than a fact.
