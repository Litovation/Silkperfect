# Trading bots mostly fail; slow momentum endures

**The internet evidence says Silkperfect's own conclusion is right: almost no retail bot, AI agent or forecasting model has a verified record of beating buy-and-hold after costs, and the few things that do work are either institutional or slow.** Regulators' and academics' datasets show 89-97% of active retail traders losing money in every country studied, with persistent winners at roughly 1-3% of the group. Independent tests of LLM trading agents (FINSABER, StockBench, Bloomberg's contest tally), time-series foundation models (TimesFM, Chronos) and open-source bot frameworks find no durable edge, and no framework (Freqtrade, Hummingbot, QuantConnect and others) publishes a verified aggregate live track record. Bots do make money in a narrow set of places: Indian prop and foreign-investor desks (99% of their derivatives profit came from algo entities in FY26), prediction-market and on-chain arbitrage, and liquidity provision, all of which depend on speed, infrastructure or subsidy and decay as competition arrives. The one method with centuries of multi-asset evidence that a small team can run is price momentum, held for months, which is exactly what Silkperfect found. The new evidence mostly agrees with the Silkperfect report, corrects one figure (the "Rs 61,000 crore" prop profit is FY24, and a newer SEBI FY26 study exists), and adds two real warnings: a t-stat of 1.5-1.8 is far short of the 3.0 bar for newly claimed factors, and one skeptical Indian backtest says momentum fails in exactly the liquid stocks Silkperfect used. The next tests should therefore aim to prove the momentum edge wrong before they try to improve it. This is research synthesis, not financial advice.

## Retail traders, AI agents and forecasting models almost never beat buy-and-hold

The cleanest evidence is about people rather than software. In Brazil, **97% of equity-futures day traders who persisted more than 300 days lost money**, and only 1.1% out-earned the minimum wage ([SSRN abstract via search](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers....act_id%3D3423101); [BloombergQuint](https://www.bloombergquint.com/onweb/day-traders-in-brazil-study-would-ve-done-better-with-9-to-5-job)). In Taiwan, aggregate day-trader performance was negative in each of 15 years and **fewer than 1% could predictably earn positive returns net of fees** ([EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:finmar:v:18:y:2014:i:c:p:1-24)). French regulators found 89% of retail FX/CFD clients lost ([Finance Magnates](https://www.financemagnates.com/forex/analysis/amf-publishes-four-year-fx-study-89-of-investors-experiencing-losses/)). In India, SEBI's cash-segment study found 71% of individual intraday traders lost money in FY23, rising to about 80% for those making more than 500 trades a year ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/markets/news/7-in-10-intraday-traders-in-equity-cash-suffered-losses-in-fy23-sebi-study-124072400975_1.html)), and its F&O series shows 89% losing in FY22, 91.1% in FY24 and about 91% in FY25 ([Business Standard](https://business-standard.com/markets/news/over-93-retail-traders-lost-money-in-f-o-in-three-years-says-sebi-124092300853_1.html); [Moneylife](https://moneylife.in/article/91-percentage-of-retail-traders-lost-money-in-derivatives-losses-in-fo-surged-41-percentage-to-rs105-lakh-crore-in-fy25-sebi-study/77613.html)). No study found splits retail accounts into algorithmic and manual, so these numbers do not prove bots lose, but the mechanism they document (loss-makers pay far more in costs relative to profits, 57% of losses versus 19% of profits in the intraday study) is the same one that sank all 64 of Silkperfect's day-trade variants.

LLM trading agents have the weakest evidence of any category. TradingAgents' paper reports cumulative returns above 23% and Sharpe ratios of 5-8, but on three stocks over roughly three months, by the authors' own backtest ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.20138), snippet-level); its own README, which was read directly, publishes no returns and calls itself a "research scaffold rather than a strategy" ([GitHub](https://github.com/TauricResearch/TradingAgents)). A maintainer there conceded that leakage from the model's weights "can't" be removed by a code change ([issue #805](https://github.com/TauricResearch/TradingAgents/issues/805)). The best independent test, FINSABER (KDD 2026), ran FinMem, FinAgent and others against buy-and-hold on about 20 years of S&P 500 data with realistic costs and found the LLM advantages "deteriorate significantly", with buy-and-hold significantly ahead on paired tests ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.07078v3); [README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/waylonli/FINSABER/main/README.md); table values are snippet-level). StockBench found most frontier LLM agents failed to beat passive holding over 82 trading days ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.02209v1)). Live contests tell the same story: in nof1's Alpha Arena Season 1, Qwen3-Max finished about +22% while GPT-5 and Gemini 2.5 Pro lost more than 55% over 17 days, a spread the organiser's founder attributed partly to luck ([Forklog](https://forklog.com/en/four-out-of-six-ai-models-suffer-losses-in-trading-tournament/), press figures that differ by outlet), and Bloomberg's May 2026 review, citing a tracker of 11 public contests, found only 2 had a profitable median model ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-05-06/ai-bots-auditioning-for-wall-street-trading-are-mostly-losing?srnd=all), snippet-only). With six models and two weeks, someone must finish first; that is not skill.

Forecasting models line up with Silkperfect's NSE result. An independent preprint found zero-shot Chronos-large and TimesFM had **out-of-sample R-squared of about -1.4% and -2.8%** on daily excess returns and trailed gradient-boosted trees ([arXiv 2511.18578](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.18578v1), snippet-only), and a second found gains over a random walk "small and sparse" ([arXiv 2606.27100](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.27100)). Kronos's claimed 93% rank-IC improvement is the authors' own benchmark on Chinese A-shares, and its README (read) states there are no backtest numbers and that the demo is "not a production-ready" system ([GitHub](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/shiyu-coder/Kronos/HEAD/README.md)); no independent replication was found. Qlib's own table (read) shows LightGBM beating LSTM, Transformer and TabNet, with annualised returns of only 0.078-0.116 on CSI300 ([GitHub](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/microsoft/qlib/main/examples/benchmarks/README.md)), so Silkperfect's 13.3% Qlib result on NSE was not an implementation failure but the framework's expected scale.

The software frameworks themselves carry no verified record. Freqtrade (57.2k stars) tells users its default strategy "will most likely not be profitable" and warns that fantastic backtests often fail live ([GitHub](https://github.com/freqtrade/freqtrade); doc wording is snippet-level), Hummingbot publishes $34 billion of user volume but not profit ([GitHub](https://github.com/hummingbot/hummingbot)), and NautilusTrader warns live execution "may not reproduce" simulated behaviour ([GitHub](https://github.com/nautechsystems/nautilus_trader)). None of Freqtrade, Hummingbot or Nautilus supports Indian equity brokers. Public "profit" evidence for commercial bots such as 3Commas and Pionex is vendor marketing or self-selected Trustpilot ratings of service quality, not trading results ([Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/Pionex.com), snippet-level). Finally, enforcement shows how the "AI bot" pitch is used: the SEC alleged a bot program with 98% "accuracy" claims had no actual bots and took at least $15.3 million ([Crypto Times](https://www.cryptotimes.io/2026/09/30/sec-charges-entities-over-alleged-15m-crypto-and-ai-investment-fraud/), allegations not adjudicated), and SEBI impounded about Rs 546 crore from the Avadhut Sathe trading academy ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/markets/news/avadhut-sathe-finfluencer-sebi-ban-disgorgement-unregistered-advisory-125120500178_1.html)).

## Where bots do earn money, the edge is speed, structure or subsidy, and it decays

The profit pool is real but captured by a small, advantaged group. SEBI's FY26 study, as reported, shows proprietary traders with Rs 44,483 crore of gross derivatives profit and foreign portfolio investors with Rs 13,896 crore, with **99% of that profit coming from algo entities** ([Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/markets/story/proprietary-traders-derivatives-profits-fall-in-fy26-says-sebi-study-550558-2026-08-21)). The notes stress that "algo entity" means any entity with at least one algo order, so this describes institutional co-located firms, not retail bots on a broker API. Jane Street's July 2025 interim order, with Rs 4,843.57 crore impounded over alleged Bank Nifty expiry manipulation and still before the tribunal in October 2026, is the sharpest illustration of what that game looks like ([Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/markets/story/sebi-tells-sat-that-jane-street-has-enough-data-to-respond-to-market-manipulation-charges-559953-2026-10-06)).

Outside India the pattern repeats. On Polymarket, one analysis found 84.1% of roughly 2.5 million addresses unprofitable and only 0.033% earning over $100,000 ([Sergeenkov](https://sergeenkov.com/polymarket-profitability/)); Bloomberg reported bots were about 5% of wallets but 75% of volume ([Bloomberg](https://bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-04-28/most-prediction-market-traders-are-losing-money-while-bots-rack-up-gains)); and an academic study estimated about $40 million of realised arbitrage over a year, mostly 1-5% opportunities ([arXiv 2508.03474](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.03474)). In on-chain sandwich trading, extraction fell from nearly $10 million a month to about $2.5 million within a year, with about 70% tied to one entity ([Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/exclusive-data-from-eigenphi-reveals-that-sandwich-attacks-on-ethereum-have-waned)). Crypto funding-rate carry is not risk-free even before costs ([arXiv 2212.06888](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.06888v5)), and a 2026 paper reportedly found only about 40% of top opportunities were profitable after costs ([MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2227-7390/14/2/346), snippet-only). Plain grid trading has roughly zero expected value before fees, per a June 2025 preprint ([arXiv 2506.11921](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.11921v1), not peer reviewed). The common thread is that survivors are paid for providing liquidity or risk transfer, or for being faster, not for predicting prices.

The crowd-sourced experiments are the sobering counterweight. Quantopian's study of 888 algorithms found backtest Sharpe explained almost none of live Sharpe (R-squared below about 0.025), and more backtesting widened the gap ([Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/quantopians-academic-paper-about-in-vs-out-of-sample-performance-of-trading-alg/)); its crowd-sourced fund was reported by Bloomberg to have lagged the S&P 500 by 62 points over five years before shutting down in 2020 ([Bloomberg](https://prod.cm.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2020-12-16/quant-trading-platform-quantopian-closes-down), snippet-only). Numerai's self-reported fund results (25.45% net in 2024, Sharpe 2.75) are the best "crowd ML made money" signal but are company claims without audit ([Numerai blog](https://blog.numer.ai/jpmorgan-secures-500m-capacity/)), and Medallion's roughly 39% net record is unaudited and closed to outsiders ([Morningstar](https://www.morningstar.com/columns/rekenthaler-report/medallion-fund-is-like-no-other)). Gu, Kelly and Xiu's peer-reviewed result that machine learning predicts US returns using mostly momentum, liquidity and volatility signals ([NBER](https://nber.org/papers/w25398)) is gross of costs and supports momentum more than it supports a model-based recipe. No audited, multi-year, self-taught retail algo track record turned up anywhere.

## Momentum is the best-supported method, but its Indian evidence is split

Momentum is the only method that clears the bar of long, multi-asset, out-of-sample evidence. Data back to 1800 across equities, currencies, bonds and commodities show significant gross momentum in every class except commodity spot prices ([Alpha Architect summary](https://alphaarchitect.com/the-worlds-longest-multi-asset-momentum-investing-backtest/)), and costs from live institutional trading are under a tenth of older academic assumptions, with momentum surviving them while short-term reversal does not ([Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/surprise-the-size-value-and-momentum-anomalies-survive-after-trading-costs/); the authors run a momentum business and a 2020 critique is more skeptical, [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jfinec/v137y2020i2p515-549.html)). Silkperfect's result that monthly rebalancing beat weekly and daily, with costs of Rs 18.7, 39.0 and 86.5 lakh, fits this literature. Its shape also fits the known failure mode: Daniel and Moskowitz show momentum is negatively skewed and crashes in "panic" rebounds after market declines ([NBER](https://www.nber.org/papers/w20439)), which matches the 2025-26 lag after a flat market (equal-weight +4.9% in 2025, -1.1% in 2026). That timing is a hypothesis to test in the data, not a finding.

The best-supported fix is volatility management. Barroso and Santa-Clara report that scaling momentum by its own recent volatility "virtually eliminates crashes and nearly doubles the Sharpe ratio" ([CFA Institute blog](https://blogs.cfainstitute.org/blog/2018/01/17/timing-the-market-momentum-and-beyond), snippet-level), though almost all such tests are long-short and gross, not long-only Indian retail. Residual momentum raises Sharpe but may not cut crash risk ([Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/strategies/residual-momentum-factor)). For a long-only book, low volatility is the natural partner: Nifty500 Low Volatility 50 reportedly returned 17.5% a year versus 14.2% for the Nifty 500 from 2005 ([Axis MF](https://www.axismf.com/mutual-fund-knowledge-centre/articles/low-volatility-index-vs-nifty-500-index), an AMC source), and one practitioner found low volatility the only factor unaffected by turnover ([freefincal](https://freefincal.com/does-factor-investing-outperformance-fail-for-high-turnover-stocks/), snippet-only).

The Indian record is where the evidence turns against comfort. Academic work generally finds momentum: a 3,956-stock BSE study for 2000-2021 found significant intermediate-term momentum amplified by liquidity ([IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/pacfin/v82y2023ics0927538x23002640.html)), though a 39-country study found it insignificant for India ([core.ac.uk summary](https://core.ac.uk/download/154383453.pdf), snippet-only). NSE's April 2026 whitepaper reports Nifty200 Momentum 30 at 19.19% a year versus 15.08% for the Nifty since 2005, but most of that is pre-launch backtest ([NSE](https://www.niftyindices.com/docs/default-source/indices/nifty200-momentum-30/momentum-strategy-whitepaper_2026.pdf), snippet-only). Live data is unflattering: the index dashboard shows weak one-year returns in late 2026 (about -8.5% in October, with unconfirmed column labels, [NSE](https://niftyindices.com/Index_Dashboard/Index_Dashboard_OCT2026.pdf)), and the momentum index funds show negative one-year figures with an SBI fund's drawdown still unrecovered in August 2026 ([IndMoney](https://www.indmoney.com/mutual-funds/sbi-nifty200-momentum-30-index-fund-direct-growth-1051814), aggregator snippet). The sharpest challenge is a March 2026 freefincal backtest in which high-turnover (liquid) momentum stocks returned 8.51% net a year over about 19 years versus 10.41% for the Nifty 50, with a -75% maximum drawdown ([freefincal](https://freefincal.com/is-indian-momentum-investing-just-a-liquidity-illusion/), a single blog backtest that contradicts the peer-reviewed liquidity finding). Silkperfect's top-200-by-turnover universe is exactly that high-turnover bucket, and its 26-41% falls come from a window that omits any 2008-style crash.

Two structural tax and cost facts favour holding longer and favour funds. Short-term equity gains are taxed at 20% and long-term at 12.5% above Rs 1.25 lakh ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/budget/news/budget-2026-ltcg-tax-rates-unchanged-for-fy27-fm-sitharaman-126020100424_1.html)), and Nifty200 Momentum 30 funds carry expense ratios around 0.3-0.5% (snippet-level fund listings, conflicting by site), with fund turnover reported at 124% a year and not taxed until redemption ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/finance/news/momentum-funds-may-deliver-high-long-term-returns-for-aggressive-investors-125070300678_1.html), snippet-only). Published anomalies also decay after publication ([Fortune on McLean-Pontiff](https://fortune.com/2012/11/02/academics-academia-destroys-stock-picking-strategies/)); the notes' working assumption is that a third to a half of an in-sample edge may not survive, though the published decay percentages were not confirmed.

## What this means for the Silkperfect report and what to test next

**Where the evidence agrees.** Every pillar of the project's conclusions is supported. AI forecasters losing to holding matches the independent TSFM and FINSABER findings. Day trading failing on a roughly 0.10% gross edge against about 0.25% cost matches SEBI's 71%/80% loss rates and Alpha Arena's finding that fees from over-trading dominated early losses ([Forklog](https://forklog.com/en/four-out-of-six-ai-models-suffer-losses-in-trading-tournament/)). Chinese rule-based timing trailing buy-and-hold, with smaller drawdowns only from sitting in cash, fits the generic finding that rules which do not carry a risk premium have no edge to find. Recommending a low-cost fund as the base and momentum as an add-on agrees with how the evidence ranks things.

**Where the evidence corrects or contradicts it.** First, the project report's "about Rs 61,000 crore in a year" matches the FY24 sum of roughly Rs 33,000 crore prop profit and Rs 28,000 crore FPI profit ([ThePrint](https://theprint.in/economy/individual-traders-incurred-cumulative-net-loss-of-rs-1-8-lakh-crore-in-derivatives-market-in-3-yrs/2280936), snippet-level), not FY25, while the "91%" figure it pairs with is FY25. The notes could not find a stated FY25 prop figure (a back-calculation from FY26's "-3%" implies about Rs 45,800 crore, which is mine to flag as unverified). A newer SEBI study released August 2026 shows 87.7% of individuals losing in FY26, aggregate net loss Rs 91,685 crore (the first fall in the series), prop gross profit Rs 44,483 crore and FPI Rs 13,896 crore, about Rs 58,400 crore combined ([Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/personal-finance/story/87-7-of-fo-traders-lost-money-in-fy26-despite-sebi-curbs-why-retail-investors-keep-losing-567419-2026-11-14); SEBI's PDFs were not opened and outlets differ on trader counts). The report should label each figure with its year, say these are gross profits of entities, and drop "mostly index options" unless sourced. Second, the report's reading that momentum is "well known and robust" needs a caveat: its t-stat of 1.5-1.8 over about 5.75 years implies an active Sharpe of only about 0.63-0.75 (my arithmetic, t divided by the square root of years), and reaching Harvey, Liu and Zhu's proposed t of 3.0 ([NBER](https://nber.org/papers/w20592)) at that rate needs roughly 16-23 years. The multi-century literature, not the local backtest, supplies the credibility. Third, the freefincal result and NSE's own weak recent live numbers mean the "ahead in all ten settings" robustness claim is a statement about one favourable start date, since the +86.9% first year dominates. Fourth, the project's plan to connect Upstox through OpenAlgo needs updating: since 1 April 2026 broker APIs require a registered static IP, and at under 10 orders per second no exchange algo registration is needed ([Zerodha support](https://support.zerodha.com/category/trading-and-markets/general-kite/kite-api/articles/static-ip); [Kite forum](https://kite.trade/forum/discussion/comment/52133/), snippet-level), so monthly or quarterly rebalancing is compliant but a home IP that changes is a new outage risk. Selling signals or running others' money would need provider or research-analyst status, a point the notes could not resolve for own-account use. Fifth, API fees (Rs 500 a month at Zerodha, free elsewhere) are negligible next to the Rs 20-45 per-order cost that wrecked the Rs 10,000 test, which agrees with the report ([Zerodha support](https://support.zerodha.com/category/trading-and-markets/kite-api/articles/what-are-the-charges-for-kite-apis)).

**Prioritised tests.** The list below is ordered by how much it could change the decision to continue with momentum, using only data Silkperfect already has or can download free.

| Rank | Test | Why the evidence points here | Decision rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Keep a trial register, then compute a deflated Sharpe ratio, a stationary-bootstrap confidence interval and a permutation test (random 20-of-200 picks) on the existing series; drop 2021 and recompute; stress costs 2x and 3x | Backtest Sharpe barely predicts live Sharpe (Quantopian); an illustrative deflated Sharpe in the notes falls from 0.85 to 0.57 as trials rise from 10 to 200, under assumed inputs, so the real count matters ([Bailey and Lopez de Prado](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2460551), snippet-only) | If the edge is not distinguishable from lucky picking at the honest trial count, treat momentum as a prior from the literature, not a local finding |
| 2 | Extend history to 2010 or earlier from NSE bhavcopy archives and split by turnover tercile | Directly tests the freefincal contradiction (liquid momentum 8.51% net) and adds 2011-13 and 2018-20 regimes | If the liquid tercile loses to the index over the long sample, stop DIY stock picking and use a fund |
| 3 | Audit point-in-time universe and delisting treatment | A 2026 study found current-members-only backtests overstated Nifty Smallcap 250 returns by about 4.94 points a year ([arXiv 2603.19380](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19380), snippet-only) | Rebuild the top-200 list from each month's files, with realistic exits for suspended or delisted names |
| 4 | Volatility-scaled or beta-capped momentum sleeve | Best-supported crash fix; uses prices only | Adopt only if it helps across several lookbacks and the 2025-26 lag, with few parameters fixed in advance |
| 5 | Momentum blended with low volatility | Low volatility has the cheapest products and best turnover robustness in the sources; targets the 26-41% drawdowns | Compare 2021 and 2025-26 separately; expect lower upside |
| 6 | Real-world comparison: DIY momentum after 20% short-term tax and costs versus a Nifty200 Momentum 30 fund | Fund costs 0.3-0.5% with untaxed internal turnover; live fund record is short and weak | If DIY does not clear the fund by a clear margin after tax, use the fund |
| 7 | Freeze rules and paper-trade forward with a dated register | Six months carries almost no statistical information; roughly three years are needed to trust a true Sharpe of 1 at 95% confidence (my derivation from the minimum-track-record formula, quoted from memory in the notes, so verify it) | Treat as an operations check, not proof |
| 8 | Simple market trend filter (one or two settings) | Weakest independent evidence, cheap to run | Reject if it only works at one lookback |
| 9 | Deferred: quality and value (needs accounts data), an LLM agent in forward paper trading only, Numerai as a skills side project, options | No India evidence for the agent or Numerai; options are where SEBI shows 92% of retail losses | Low priority; the LLM agent would need 6-12 months of costed forward logging to say anything |

Tests 4 to 8 multiply the number of variants tried, so they should be few, declared before running, and counted in the register from test 1; an AI coding agent that tries variants without a trial log would repeat the p-hacking the preprint literature warns about ([arXiv 2608.27734](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.27734), snippet-only).

## Conclusion

The research changes what "success" means for this project. The documented winners are not bots that predicted prices; they are firms paid for speed, liquidity or risk transfer, and a slow risk premium that anyone can hold through a cheap fund. For Silkperfect, the practical question is therefore not "which bot should we run" but "is our momentum edge real in liquid Indian shares, and is it worth the tax and effort over a fund". The strongest new argument against complacency comes from inside India, from the liquid-momentum result and the weak recent live momentum funds, while the strongest argument for patience is that crashes and long lulls are documented features of momentum, not signs of failure.

Three gaps limit confidence and are worth closing before any money is committed: the primary SEBI, NSE, SSRN and arXiv documents were never opened, no independent evidence exists on how retail algo users in India fare, and the community layer (Reddit, Hacker News) was not read. Re-running the key checks with those hosts reachable, starting with the SEBI FY26 PDFs and the NSE whitepaper, would convert the most important snippet-level numbers into verified ones. Nothing here is financial advice.
