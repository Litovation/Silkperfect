# Chinese trading recipes fail the NSE test

None of the openly available Chinese methods examined here has reliable published proof of making money after costs, and in this project's own test on 200 NSE shares (February 2021 to November 2026, full delivery costs) every one of them earned less than simply holding the same shares. Holding earned **15.7% a year**; czsc's Chan-theory buy and sell points earned 4.1% to 11.7%, RSRS timing 5.7%, turtle breakout 3.9%, and the one-month reversal that works in China earned 7.7% against 16.1% for holding all shares equally ([project test](chinese_methods.md)). The tools themselves are real, free and mostly safe to use: czsc is Apache-2.0 licensed, installs without any Chinese data source and runs on any daily price table ([czsc LICENSE](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/waditu/czsc/master/LICENSE), [PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/czsc/)). What is missing is evidence. The czsc author publishes no performance figures and tells users to validate every signal themselves ([README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/waditu/czsc/master/README.md)); the careful small-creator tests that include costs show their strategies losing to buy-and-hold; and the big return claims (96% to 135% a year for small-cap strategies) appear only in post titles on sites that sell strategy code. The one result worth following up is not Chinese at all: ordinary 12-month momentum earned 25.8% a year in the project test, though its statistical strength (1.73) fell short of the usual pass mark of 2. Of the Chinese ideas, only the Chan "third-class" buy point and a few price-only ranking factors deserve another look, and only as ways to reduce the size of falls, not to beat holding.

## czsc turns price charts into zigzags, then into 246 named signals

Chan theory (缠论) comes from a series of blog posts by a writer called 缠中说禅, and czsc is the best-known open Python library that automates it. Its author describes it as a tool that starts from price bars and identifies "fractals, strokes and pivot zones", then organises signals and backtests ([README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/waditu/czsc/master/README.md)). In plain terms: the library first tidies the daily bars, then marks local tops and bottoms (fractals), joins them into up and down legs (strokes, at least 6 bars long by default), and finds price ranges where several legs overlap (pivot zones, meaning sideways areas). On top of this it defines three kinds of buy point. A **first-class** point is a bet that a falling trend has run out of force. A **second-class** point is the first pullback after that which does not make a new low. A **third-class** point is when price has broken out of a sideways area and the first dip stays above it, which is a bet that a trend will continue. These plain-language definitions are standard Chan-theory background mapped onto the czsc objects; the exact coded rule inside each czsc signal was not read by the researchers.

Three properties matter for anyone testing it. First, czsc is one person's interpretation, not the theory itself: the author's FAQ says the library skips the "segment" layer of the original theory and measures a move's force by its price range for speed ([Discussion #32](https://github.com/waditu/czsc/discussions/32)), and each buy point exists in several dated versions. Second, the most recent stroke is provisional and gets redrawn as new prices arrive, so a chart drawn afterwards looks cleaner than what a trader saw on the day; only signals generated bar by bar are honest. Third, the library moves fast and breaks old code. Version **1.0.2 was uploaded on 16 October 2026**, needs Python 3.10 or later, and ships as a ready-built 19–23 MB package with no Chinese data dependency ([PyPI JSON](https://pypi.org/pypi/czsc/json), [pyproject.toml](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/waditu/czsc/master/pyproject.toml)). The 1.0 line is not compatible with 0.9, and 1.0.2 itself renamed 34 signal outputs with no fallback, so old strategies silently stop matching ([README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/waditu/czsc/master/README.md), [CHANGELOG](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/waditu/czsc/master/CHANGELOG.md)). The researchers installed 1.0.2 and ran it end to end on made-up daily data: it produced 246 catalogued signals, 43 of them Chan-structure signals, nearly all usable on daily bars; it keeps only the last 50 strokes unless told otherwise; its default trading fee of 0.02% is far below Indian delivery costs and must be set by hand; and trades are booked at the same day's closing price that triggered them, which flatters results. That run was on random data and says nothing about profit. It was never run on real NSE data by the researchers; the project's own test below is the only NSE evidence.

One alternative Chan library exists, chan.py by Vespa314 (MIT licence). It does include the segment layer, but only about 5,300 of roughly 22,000 lines are public; the strategy and scoring parts are withheld, and the author himself says the framework does not prove Chan theory is useful ([chan.py](https://github.com/Vespa314/chan.py)).

## Beyond czsc: many repositories, few complete rules, some licence traps

Most famous Chinese quant projects are plumbing (data storage, backtest engines) with only toy examples. The repositories below contain actual trading rules. Licences were read from the repository pages; "none shown" means no licence was visible, which by default grants no right to reuse the code, although re-implementing a published idea in your own code is a separate matter.

| Project (author) | What it gives you | Licence | Usable on NSE daily data? |
|---|---|---|---|
| [czsc](https://github.com/waditu/czsc) (zengbin93) | Chan-theory structure and 246 signals | Apache-2.0 | Yes, already tested by this project |
| [chan.py](https://github.com/Vespa314/chan.py) (Vespa314) | Chan structure and typed buy/sell points; strategy layer closed | MIT | Yes, but you write the strategy |
| [vnpy CTA examples](https://github.com/vnpy/vnpy_ctastrategy/tree/main/vnpy_ctastrategy/strategies) (VeighNa team) | Turtle, Bollinger+CCI, double moving average, ATR+RSI, Keltner, Dual Thrust | MIT on main repo; plugin licence not shown | Yes on the buy side, except Dual Thrust (intraday) |
| [myhhub/stock](https://github.com/myhhub/stock) | 11 chart-pattern screens, 61 candlestick patterns | Apache-2.0 | Partly; many rules assume China's 10% daily limit and have no exit rule |
| [Sequoia-X](https://github.com/sngyai/Sequoia) (sngyai) | 6 after-close screens | MIT | Partly; two depend on China's limit-up mechanism |
| [QuantsPlaybook](https://github.com/hugo2046/QuantsPlaybook) (hugo2046) | About 60 replications of Chinese brokerage research, including RSRS | None shown | Ideas yes; code needs Chinese data services |
| [rsrs-timing](https://github.com/cedric-runtaoxu/rsrs-timing) (cedric-runtaoxu) | RSRS replication with cost scenarios | MIT | Yes, already tested by this project |
| [a-share-multifactor-backtest](https://github.com/osnddwqd-hub/a-share-multifactor-backtest) (osnddwqd-hub) | Four ranking factors with honest out-of-sample test | MIT (code) | Three of four factors need prices only |
| [Qbot](https://github.com/UFund-Me/Qbot) (Charmve) | Long list of strategy names, few rules | Conflicting: MIT badge, README says CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 | Avoid for reuse |
| [abu](https://github.com/bbfamily/abu) (bbfamily) | Signal framework including Chan points | GPL-3.0 | Copyleft; no parameters published |
| [alphagen](https://github.com/RL-MLDM/alphagen), [AlphaForge](https://github.com/DulyHao/AlphaForge) | AI that searches for price formulas | None shown | Needs a data adapter and heavy computing |
| [TradingAgents-CN](https://github.com/hsliuping/TradingAgents-CN) (hsliuping) | AI agents that write research reports | Part Apache-2.0, part proprietary | No; text output, nothing to test |

The best-specified rules work as follows. The **turtle breakout** in vnpy buys when price exceeds its 20-bar high, adds up to three more units as price rises by half an average daily range each time, and sells at the 10-bar low or two average ranges below the last purchase ([source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/vnpy/vnpy_ctastrategy/main/vnpy_ctastrategy/strategies/turtle_signal_strategy.py)). Confusingly, the stock screeners use "turtle" to mean only "today's close is the highest of the last 60 days" (myhhub) or 20 days (Sequoia-X), with no exit. **RSRS**, from a 2017 Chinese brokerage report, measures how fast daily highs rise relative to daily lows over 18 days, compares that with its own 600-day history, and holds when the score is above 0.7 and sells below −0.7; these settings come from second-hand write-ups because the original report was not read, and they were picked as the best of a scan on one Chinese index ([replication](https://github.com/cedric-runtaoxu/rsrs-timing)). **Slope × R² rotation** ranks funds by how steeply and how smoothly they have been rising and holds the top one ([etf-signal](https://github.com/solary2014/etf-signal)). The four-factor repository ranks shares by momentum from 60 to 20 days ago, by the reverse of the last 5 days' move, by low volatility and by low turnover, and holds the top few for a month ([source](https://github.com/osnddwqd-hub/a-share-multifactor-backtest)). The heavily promoted **small-cap** strategy cannot be tested on price data alone, since it needs company size and accounting filters, and a list of the 200 most traded shares excludes the tiny companies it relies on. The AI projects divide cleanly: formula searchers produce something testable but demand serious computing and state no hardware needs, while agent systems produce prose and say outright that they give no price targets or timing instructions ([TradingAgents-CN](https://github.com/hsliuping/TradingAgents-CN)).

## The more careful the test, the weaker the result

Published evidence follows one pattern. Projects with large audiences make no numeric claims; the vnpy examples, myhhub/stock, Sequoia-X, alphagen and AlphaForge show none on their pages. The numbers that do exist come from tiny personal repositories, and the two that included costs and a held-back test period both lost to buy-and-hold. The four-factor strategy made **18.5% over 2024–2025 against 35.1% for the CSI 300 index**, with its author stating plainly that no excess return was shown ([source](https://github.com/osnddwqd-hub/a-share-multifactor-backtest)). A grid-trading test on a Chinese dividend fund from 2019 to 2026 made 5% to 18% in total against **76% for holding** ([grid-backtest](https://github.com/kapibaLa7/grid-backtest)). RSRS looks strong in replication, about 22% a year against 11% for holding the CSI 300, but that table ends in April 2017, leaves out trading costs, and uses settings chosen on the same data ([rsrs-timing](https://github.com/cedric-runtaoxu/rsrs-timing)). At the other end, QuantsPlaybook prints round averages with no period or costs, and another repository prints a table of winners with no method ([QuantsPlaybook](https://github.com/hugo2046/QuantsPlaybook), [awesome-quant-strategies](https://github.com/testtestme123/awesome-quant-strategies)). Those are not evidence.

For Chan theory specifically, the researchers found nothing that was at once numerical, after costs, tested on held-back data and independent. The only figures attached to czsc are guesses in a user's GitHub issue with no backtest behind them ([Issue #353](https://github.com/waditu/czsc/issues/353)). A brokerage-style report and a 2019 journal article on Chan theory exist but could not be opened, so their numbers are unknown. Academic work on simpler chart rules is not encouraging: a study of moving-average and breakout rules on Chinese indexes from 1992 to 2013 found that once costs are counted, "trading profits will be eliminated completely" ([Zhu et al., 2015](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1504.04254)). One later study of 28,000 signals claims its results survive costs, but only its abstract was available ([Jiang et al., 2019](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/irvfin/v19y2019i1p191-206.html)).

What Chinese shares do reliably show is different from what the tools sell. Individual Chinese A-shares tend to **reverse over one month** instead of continuing, a pattern researchers link to a market dominated by small individual traders; the same companies' foreign-traded B-shares show the opposite ([Review of Finance](https://revfin.org/momentum-reversals-and-investor-clientele/)). When 469 known patterns were retested in China without over-weighting tiny companies, **about 83% failed** ([Li et al., 2024](https://ira.lib.polyu.edu.hk/bitstream/10397/115240/1/Li_Replicating_Digesting_Anomalies.pdf)). China's professional funds do beat their indexes, by roughly 9% to 17% a year in 2021–2025 according to sales-platform data, but they hold thousands of shares for under ten days with turnover around 100 times a year, their edge collapsed in February 2024 when the smallest shares fell about 31% in five days, and it shrank to 0.85% on the CSI 500 index in the first half of 2026 ([Tencent News](https://news.qq.com/rain/a/20231206A00DQO00), [Qatar Tribune/Reuters](https://qatar-tribune.com/article/109660/business/chinese-hedge-funds-struggle-to-calm-investors-amid-losses), [Tencent News, June 2026](https://news.qq.com/rain/a/20260622A08S2T00)). Most of these fund figures came from search summaries, not full articles, and should be treated as approximate. An individual trading daily prices is not doing what those funds do.

No study of any of these methods on Indian shares was found. The nearest clue points the wrong way: a hobby project found that a standard Chinese price-and-volume model that worked in China in 2026 paper trading had almost no predictive power on US shares ([OpenTrade](https://github.com/Holidayrabbit/OpenTrade)).

## On 200 NSE shares, every Chinese rule trailed plain holding

The project's own test fills that gap. It took the 200 most traded NSE shares as they stood in February 2021, gave each Rs 500,000, applied each rule share by share with full delivery costs, and compared the result with simply holding the same shares until November 2026. Cash earned nothing while a rule was out of the market ([project test](chinese_methods.md)).

| Rule | Return per year | Gap to holding | Worst drop | Trades | Costs paid (Rs) | Shares where rule beat holding |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Hold the same shares | 15.7% | — | −21.4% | 200 | 520,817 | — |
| Chan: all buy and sell points | 4.4% | −11.3 | −13.7% | 8,847 | 16,426,023 | 18% |
| Chan: first-class points only | 4.1% | −11.6 | −10.9% | 662 | 1,255,493 | 25% |
| Chan: third-class points only | 11.7% | −4.0 | −13.8% | 513 | 1,130,563 | 28% |
| RSRS (18, 600, 0.7) | 5.7% | −10.0 | −11.4% | 4,898 | 9,417,698 | 16% |
| Turtle breakout (20, 10) | 3.9% | −11.8 | −12.6% | 4,415 | 8,184,987 | 16% |

Three things stand out. Every rule cut the worst drop by a third to a half, which is what a rule that sits in cash 40% to 60% of the time does automatically; that is not skill. Trading costs were punishing: using all Chan points cost Rs 16.4 million on a starting pot of Rs 100 million, about 32 times the cost of holding. And no rule beat holding on more than 28% of individual shares, so the shortfall is broad and not caused by a few unlucky names. The **third-class Chan point** is the only rule that came close: 11.7% a year while invested 63% of the time, with a risk-adjusted score (Sharpe ratio) of 1.05 against 1.01 for holding. That difference is too small to call an edge, and with three Chan variants tried, one looking best is expected by chance. It fits the wider result, though: the third-class point follows trends, and NSE shares in this period rewarded trend-following and punished betting on turnarounds. The first-class point, which the unverified Chinese brokerage summary called the best performer in China, was the weakest of the three Chan variants here.

The stock-picking test says the same thing more sharply. Buying each month the 20 shares that fell most, the pattern that works in China, earned **7.7% a year against 16.1%** for holding all 200 equally. Buying the past year's winners (12-month momentum, skipping the latest month) earned **25.8%**, with a deeper worst drop of −32.4% and a strength score of 1.73, below the pass mark of 2 ([project test](chinese_methods.md)). India behaved the opposite way to China in this sample.

## Conclusion

The Chinese toolbox is worth more as software than as strategy. czsc, vnpy's examples and the small MIT-licensed replications are free, legal to use and technically sound, but they describe patterns tuned on a market with daily price limits, next-day-only selling and a crowd of small traders who create one-month reversals. NSE's 200 most traded shares showed momentum instead, so recipes built to fade moves or to dodge in and out lost to patience. Nothing published contradicts that; the absence of honest after-cost evidence in China is itself a finding.

For further testing, the order of priority follows from the evidence. Momentum deserves the most work, because it is the only result near significance; the Chinese slope × R² ranking and the "momentum from 60 to 20 days ago" factor are cheap variants of the same idea and need prices only. The Chan third-class point is worth one more test as a filter for when to hold a momentum pick, with the version pinned to czsc 1.0.2, trades placed the day after the signal, and the last two years held back untouched. Low volatility is the remaining untested price-only factor. RSRS on individual shares, turtle breakout, first-class Chan points, one-month reversal, grid trading, small-cap rotation and the AI agent systems can be dropped: each either failed here, cannot be tested on this data, or has no honest evidence behind it. One open question the test cannot answer is whether any of this holds outside 2021–2026, a period in which Indian shares mostly rose; a rule that sits in cash will look worse in such a stretch than it would across a crash.
